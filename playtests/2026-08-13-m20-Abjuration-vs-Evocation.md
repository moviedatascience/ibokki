## Analysis

PILOTED CALIBRATION (game 2 of 3): Abjuration (Claude, P0) vs greedy Evocation bot, seed 6102, NEW-DECK resource construction (15 primary basics, 8 same-symbol duals, cross-duals split by demand — Abj got 2 VS + 6 SM, Evo got 6 VM). RESULT: WIN, round 12. Final HP P0:22, P1:-16. The old-deck ward-stack/Stone-Stance-first/Reckoning line (m8-m10) survives on the new decks essentially unchanged in structure, and the edge still reads as dominant, not 50/50.

EXCHANGE RATE — STONE STANCE: cast in 11 of 12 rounds (every round had fuel except none were fully skipped, but R10 was cast LATE — after Meteor already landed unmitigated, the one clean doctrine gap this game, see below). Each cast cost exactly one S-shaped component (plain S, VS, SM, or SS/VSM overpay — multi-symbol duals fund it with zero friction, confirming "SS shapes assemble easily now" from the brief). Net effect matched m8: -2 blanked or nearly-blanked every cantrip-tier Evo cast (Spark 2→0, Firebolt 3→1, Inferno Lance 4→2) and materially discounted every L2/L3 spell (Fireball 5+1chalk→4, Chain Lightning 4→2 direct + 2 ward-only, Meteor unmitigated once).

WARD ECONOMY: peaked at 14 HP on one ward (R9-10, via 3x Fortify + 2x Bulwark Shard) plus a separate 3-13 HP ward from Aegis Charm/Reflective Ward running concurrently — confirmed multiple simultaneous Wards each independently soak and each independently feed Ward Pulse's "each Ward gains 1 HP." Reflective Ward's passive (1 dmg back per prevent) fired automatically and invisibly folded into the face-damage total on turns where I cast nothing offensive. Total wards built across the match: Fortify x9 (2 HP each), Ward Pulse x9 (1 HP + 1 dmg each, when a Ward was up), Bulwark Shard x2 (+2 each), Aegis Charm x1 (+3), Reflective Ward x1 (+3, reflect rider). Opponent ward-removal was sharper than the old-deck read: Dispelling Powder was used TWICE, and the second use (R10) was correctly sequenced immediately before Fireball to strip the ward and force the hit onto HP — a smarter play than m8's Dispelling Powder-into-nothing opener. Even so, only 8 HP of face damage got through across the whole match outside of exhaustion/burn overflow.

RECKONING CADENCE — the headline new-deck finding: unlocked on schedule at R10 (max spell level ramp is independent of round/character level and stayed capped at L1 through R4, L2 through R9, L3 from R10 — slower than the round counter, worth remembering for future pilots). First cast landed R11 for **16 damage** (P1 18→2). Second cast landed R12 for **17 damage**, finishing the game outright (P1 1→-16). The critical mechanical observation: the SECOND cast dealt MORE than the first despite only ~3-8 HP of new prevention happening in between (one Ward absorbing 3 of an 8-point burn tick). That is only possible if Reckoning's "total damage you have prevented this match" is a non-decrementing running total — each cast re-reads the full lifetime bank rather than spending it down. With 8 SS duals per side this round it funded in one card-pair with no friction either time. Practically: once Reckoning is live, a ward-stack Abj deck is not one Reckoning away from relevance, it is a two-cast kill button if the first cast doesn't already end things — this is a significant power finding worth a design look (see Pricing notes).

VERDICT ON THE EDGE: bot-level parity (53%→50/50) does NOT describe skilled play on the new decks. This piloted game reproduces the old-deck 3-0 read: Abjuration wins comfortably, taking only 8 HP of "real" damage (excluding exhaustion and the terminal Conflagration overflow that happened one turn before the kill anyway) across 12 rounds against a bot that assembled its full L3 kit (Meteor/Chain Lightning/Pyroclasm/Conflagration) on schedule. Faster Evo burst assembly (confirmed: VVV spells funded from 2 components by R10, matching the brief's "easier now" prediction) did NOT translate into a faster clock in practice, because Stone Stance + the ward wall discount and then fully absorb most of it, and Reckoning's compounding payout scales with exactly how much burst the opponent throws. If anything, a more aggressive new-deck Evo feeds the Abj win condition harder. This is piloted-series game 2 of 3 for this design cycle; consistent with m8-m10's 90%+ estimate.

NEW-DECK SHAPE EFFECTS: multi-symbol assembly was frictionless in >90% of turns — SS, VS, SM, and VSM components all fund Abjuration's mostly-S-cost kit with only mild overpay waste, exactly as the brief predicted from the 2 VS + 6 SM allocation. The one real doctrine gap: at R10 I had NO S-shaped component in hand for a full turn cycle and had to let Meteor (7 dmg) land before Stone Stance was up — the deck being easier on average doesn't eliminate single-turn variance, and a big spell landing in that exact window is a real (if infrequent) risk. On the Evo side, 6 VM duals let them fund V-heavy spells while banking spare M pips; no comparable "wrong shape stuck in hand" moment was observed for P1 this game.

RECKONING BANK LEDGER (approximate, reconstructed from visible prevention events — engine-internal, not exact): R2 Spark fully blanked (~2), R3 Firebolt SS+Grounding (~3), R4 Firebolt SS+ward (~3), R6 Fireball SS+ward (~6), R7 Inferno Lance SS+ward (~4), R10 Meteor ward-only no-SS (~7), R10 Chain Lightning SS+ward+direct-ward (~6), R10 final Fireball SS-only, ward already stripped (~2) — sums to roughly 33, consistent with the 16-damage first cast (half rounded up of ~32-33). The R12 recast for 17 off only ~3 marginal new prevention is the strongest evidence for the non-decrementing-bank read above.

BOT BLINDSPOTS / BEHAVIOR NOTES: (1) P1 hoarded a large hand (7→10, hitting the auto-discard cap at R9) for multiple full rounds before unloading everything into a stacked R10 burst turn (Meteor immediately, Chain Lightning next turn, Fireball on the following final turn) — this reads as a coherent "load for a one-shot burst window" heuristic rather than a pure blind spot, and it DID represent the bot's best offensive round of the game, it just wasn't enough. (2) Reaction-legality enforcement (Combust only answering my Reactions; my spell-triggered reactions only answering their spells) was never actually tested in this game — I held all my Reactions unfueled whenever Combust showed VV, so no illegal-reaction edge case was exercised; worth a dedicated follow-up if that enforcement needs verification. (3) Deck exhaustion hit BOTH sides this game (P1 at R10 for 2 dmg, me at R11 for 2 dmg) — unlike m8 where only the opponent hit exhaustion — suggesting the new tighter resource decks drain at a comparable rate for both schools, not just the more profligate one.

PRICING NOTES: Reckoning's non-decrementing full-match bank (if confirmed by an engine-code check, not just this game's numbers) means a ward-stack Abj deck that survives to L10 with any meaningful prevention history gets what is effectively TWO nearly-full-strength finishers, not one bank to spend once — the second cast here out-damaged the first. If that is not the intended design, consider either decrementing the bank on cast or explicitly documenting it as an intentional "the bank only grows, cast it whenever, it never runs dry" mechanic (which does read as thematically consistent with "Reckoning" but is a notably strong repeatable win condition). Everything else — Stone Stance efficiency, ward-soak-into-bank double counting, Absorb/Grounding reaction value — reads the same as the m8-m10 findings and appears untouched by the new-deck resource change.

ENGINE OBSERVATIONS: no crashes, no illegal-action prompts, no stuck states. Multiple concurrent Wards render and update correctly (`wards[a,b]` compact notation) and Ward Pulse correctly grows all of them simultaneously. Deck-empty reshuffle + exhaustion fired identically for both sides when their resource decks hit 0. Attach-action indexing by prepared-slot-position occasionally caused pilot misclicks when an earlier slot was already cast (landed a component back on an already-resolved Stone Stance twice this game) — not an engine bug, just a note for future pilots to double check the target slot index against the current `prep:` line before attaching.
