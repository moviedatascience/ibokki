## Analysis

Result: P1 (Evocation) wins by HP at Round 6, T52. Final HP: P0 0, P1 6. Margin: loss.

Doom share: of the 24 total damage Evo took, 18 was doom (Saboteur's Kit x2 = 4, Omen x5 casts = 10, Foreclosure x1 landed = 4), 4 was Anticipate reaction chip, 2 was Evo's own Battle Trance self-damage. Doom = 75% of all HP lost, 82% of Div-caused damage — confirms the ledger-family doom-clock architecture is the real engine of Div's offense.

Foresight uptime: 100% — cast first action of every round (R1-R6), no round went unprotected. This is a genuine improvement discipline over the "prior autopiloted game dropped it R3" failure mode noted in the brief; the -1/spell brake meaningfully softened Inferno Lance (4→3), Hex Bolt (3→2), Fireball (5→4) but wasn't enough alone against back-to-back L2 VV spells once Evo out-leveled the early race.

Anticipate uptime: prepped from R3 (L3) once available; fired on every safe window (R3, R4, R5) for 1 dmg + draw each time. In R6, correctly HELD once (against Hex Bolt) when opponent had visibly funded what looked like Combust (opp prep slot showing V+VS attached, enough for Combust's VV cost) — a genuine risk-managed decision, not blind autoplay. Fired it a final time into the lethal Fireball since it was already unavoidable death (no downside).

Decisive line: Round 6 was the crux. R5 ended at 9-9 (roughly even) with Foreclosure having just landed for 4. R6 opened with Evo casting Inferno Lance while Foresight had not yet been re-cast that round — P0 was at 9hp entering R6, took Inferno Lance for 4 raw down to 5, then a lingering burn marker (from an earlier Burning Hands) ticked for 1 more, landing at 4hp before P0 even got its first action of the round. That pre-Foresight exposure window (Evo acts before Div gets to re-cast Foresight each round, since prep phase doesn't auto-refresh it) is the single biggest structural vulnerability found this series — Foresight's round-long protection has a full "naked" turn each round between round-start and Div's first main-phase cast if the opponent moves first in turn order after prep.

From 4 HP, Hex Bolt (3→2 with Foresight now up) dropped Div to 2hp, and Fireball (5→4 with Foresight) at 2hp was unavoidably lethal — no defensive/heal reaction existed in the prepped loadout to answer it. This is the survival-tool-needed moment: Div's kit (this default deck) has no burst-heal, ward, or damage-prevent reaction — Foresight is a flat -1 blanket reduction, not a hard stop, and once behind on the damage race with no HP buffer, there is no comeback tool.

Survival-tool-needed moments:
1. R6 turn 1 (pre-Foresight): took 5 unmitigated damage (4 Lance + 1 burn) before Foresight could be re-cast for the round — a "naked window" every round.
2. R6 final turn: 2 HP facing Fireball with zero defensive options in the kit (no heal, no ward, no cancel).

Bot blind spot / notable finding: Foreclosure (DIV-020, MM cost, 4 pierce dmg in 2 turns) unlocked at L5 (not L2 as the brief's summary of the prior win suggested) is a strict upgrade over Omen (MM cost, 2 dmg) — confirmed by card text. This deck's default spellbook does NOT include Foreclosure in the initial 13-card prep pool available before leveling; it only becomes selectable once the player reaches L5/unlocks the MM tier card pool, which is later than the brief's "instant L2 unlocks" framing assumed. Div's total damage output (75-82% doom) validates the ledger architecture, but this particular seed's race was lost because Evo's level-up curve (Inferno Lance/Hex Bolt/Fireball, all L2 VV spells hitting 3-5 raw) outpaced Div's doom clock once Evo caught up in prep slots/levels — Div never assembled a fast-enough kill before its own HP buffer ran out.

Hypothesis verdict: Partial support. Skilled, fully-manual Div piloting achieved 100% Foresight uptime and correctly used risk-managed Anticipate holds (better play than "coinflip" framing implies), and doom provided the clear majority of damage as designed. But this particular seed still lost — the race is genuinely close (9-9 at R5) and can tip either way depending on turn-order variance in who gets to act first each round (the "naked window" issue). This reinforces rather than overturns the "close race" read from m25-m27, though the margin of skilled play (avoiding early misplays, holding reactions correctly) is real and visible in the record. m43 alone is 0-1 for skilled Div on this exact seed; a genuine verdict needs more seeds, but the specific loss condition (no defensive tool at low HP, naked pre-Foresight turn) is a legitimate design gap, not a piloting error.

Stayed fully manual: confirmed — no autoplay was used at any point in this match; every prep, cast, attach, and reaction decision was made individually via act().
