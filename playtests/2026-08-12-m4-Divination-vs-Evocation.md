## Analysis

RESULT: Divination (piloted) def. greedy Evocation, round 5, 6-0 HP. Seed 1024. Second consecutive piloted Div win over greedy Evo (seed 812 won at 2 HP; this one at 6) after sim bots went 0-240 in the matchup.

PER-CARD CONTRIBUTION (30 dmg total: 28 dealt + 2 Battle Trance self-damage):
- Dooms: 16 dmg. Omen x4 (R1 T1→fired T4; R2 T6→T9; R3 T12→T15; R4 T19→carried, fired their first R5 turn), Saboteur's Kit x2 (both drawn by Premonition/natural draw in R3, played free, fired T13/T15), Foreclosure (R5 T22→fired their T2 start for exactly-lethal 4 vs 4 HP).
- Foretell x4 (R1-R4): 8 dmg + hand intel every round (revealed their gambit-clogged, component-dry hands — informed the press decisions).
- Anticipate x4 fires (R1-R4): 4 dmg + 4 draws. R5 armed but they never cast again.
- Premonition (R3, the 5th-slot pick): drew 3 incl. GAM-019 Saboteur's Kit — the single best cast of the game; casting it BEFORE Omen on the first 3-slot round (dig, then spend) let the whole curve run that round (Kit + Omen + Foretell + Anticipate = 7 scheduled dmg in R3).
- Foresight x1 (R4 at 7 HP): blunted Spark 2→1 (est. 1 HP saved — they only landed one spell after it) and its scry fetched the CMP-MM that guaranteed Foretell. Modest but the R4 defensive posture is why I entered R5 at 6 instead of 4.

EVO BRICK ASSESSMENT: significant. They passed R2-T5 (hand of gambits/items, no V), spent their LAST R4 cast slot on Catalyst (+1 dmg round buff) with zero casts remaining to use it — a genuine bot valuation blunder — and then bricked BOTH R5 turns (hand 0, no V components), so the one danger window (their lone R5 attack turn vs my 6 HP, worst-case ~6 dmg spike) never happened. Important caveat: the kill was already forced before the brick — carried Omen (2) + Foreclosure (4) vs their 6 HP was lethal by their second R5 turn-start even with zero Anticipate/Foretell contribution, and their maximum one-turn spike left me alive at ~1. So the brick bought margin, not the win.

NEW MECHANICAL LEARNINGS (not in prior briefs):
1. Burning Hands burn ticked ONCE ("P0 burns for 1") and expired — it is not a persistent stack. Divine is not worth a prep slot against single applications.
2. Dooms carry across round boundaries and fire at the opponent's first turn-start of the new round — an end-of-round Omen is effectively a next-round opener and the cheapest way to pre-load checkmate math.
3. Anticipate structurally CANNOT fire on the opponent's first cast of a round when they have first seat (attachments swept at round end; no arm window before their T1). Expect to lose that fire every round you sit second.
4. First seat alternated ME-first on odd rounds (R1/R3/R5) this game — the "opponent moves first in odd rounds" note from the prior brief is seat-dependent, not absolute.
5. GAM-016 Sealed Vault (free): shuffles discard into resource deck — real late-game value once ~15 spent components are in the bin; zero-cost fuel-density play.
6. Bot behaviors: greedy Evo will Battle Trance itself down 2 HP for a spell boost (self-damage covered 2 of the 30) and will burn its final cast slot on a round-buff (Catalyst) it can never use. Both look like horizon/valuation blind spots worth a ledger entry.

SKILL VS LUCK READ: The win itself was skill/structure — the doom-clock playbook (always-ticking prophecy, Anticipate every window, Foretell chip, Premonition dig, Foreclosure checkmate timed to their turn-start) forced a mathematically locked kill from the R4 Foretell onward, and the same line won on a different seed last game. The 6-HP margin (vs 2 last time) was seed luck: Evo's R5 component drought and the wasted Catalyst slot. Card-power read: Foreclosure remains the matchup's kill card; Omen's cross-round carry is quietly the engine that makes the geometry work; Premonition's expected value in the 5th slot was again enormous (it found 2 free dooms across both piloted wins). Div-vs-Evo is winnable at full strength but still requires near-zero wasted M — two fires' worth of slack, no more.
