## Analysis

### RESULT: LOSS for Abjuration, round 10. Final HP: Abj -4 / Div 30.
Killed by Entropy (7 dmg doom, L3) landing on a player at 3 HP with an empty bank and no other heal source. HP path: 30→28(R1)→26(R2)→24(R3)→22(R4)→14(R5, Foreclosure+Saboteur's Kit double-doom turn)→9(R6, Foreclosure)→9(R7, held)→3(R9, Foreclosure+deck-reshuffle penalty stacked)→-4(R10, Entropy).

### Bank charge rate vs a pierce school: confirmed low but non-zero, and killable by the opponent
The assignment's core hypothesis was correct on both halves:
- **The bank CAN charge vs Divination**, but only through one narrow channel that had to be actively engineered by NOT using the standard denial doctrine: letting Foretell (the only direct-damage spell in Div's kit) resolve into a standing ward instead of sealing/cancelling it. Each Foretell that hit a ward added 2 to the prevented bank (confirmed 3 times: R4T22, R6T47, R8T62 — ward HP dropped by exactly 2 each time, no HP lost). Runic Seal-ing Foretell (the reflexive doctrine move from m22-m24) produces **zero** bank progress, because a sealed spell never resolves — there's no damage event to prevent. This is a real tension: the standard denial line and the ledger family actively fight each other for the same trigger (Foretell), and a pilot has to notice this and deliberately *not* seal Foretell to make the bank move at all. Stone Stance, prepped R3-R4 specifically to test the assignment's other flagged bank source, never once contributed — the ward was already absorbing Foretell's full 2 damage before Stone Stance's reduction could matter, making it redundant dead weight in this specific matchup (swapped out R5).
- **The bank is trivially killable by the opponent's own deck construction.** Bank hit exactly 6 three separate times (R4, R6, R8) — each time it was immediately spent (once on Sealed Verdict R8T64, see below) or simply sat at a small nonzero value. Then at R10 prep, the greedy Div bot swapped Foretell OUT of its prep list entirely (replaced by Entropy), removing the only bank-charging trigger in the whole game. From that point the bank was stuck at 0 with zero path to recharge — confirmed directly: at HP3 facing a queued lethal doom, Restoring Rune had no `cast` option available at all (verified via verbose state dump), meaning the engine gates it on prevented > 0, not just funding. This is the assignment's "MM-dense doom clock ... never touch wards/prevention" thesis playing out exactly as predicted, and it's *reactive* — Div doesn't need to plan around it, it falls out naturally once the bot leans fully into its doom kit.

### Verdict verdict: went live once, worked perfectly, then the pipeline died
Sealed Verdict was cast exactly once (R8T64), cancelling **Far Sight** (a 2-dmg fast doom) at HP9. It resolved cleanly — `Far Sight [DIV-023] CANCELLED` — proving the mechanism works exactly as designed: a bank built from chip damage funded a real answer to a real doom. That's the "one bank-funded cancel per doom round" the brief was testing for, and it happened. But:
- It only ever went live **once** in a 10-round game, despite Sealed Verdict sitting prepped and pre-funded (S) from R6 onward — the bank simply wasn't there most of the time.
- The moment it would have mattered most — R10, HP3, Entropy incoming — it (and Restoring Rune) were both dead because Div had stopped casting Foretell three prep-cycles earlier. The bank-charging trigger is entirely opponent-controlled; a Divination pilot/bot that never casts a direct-damage spell (which the greedy bot did on its own, apparently correctly identifying that Foretell revealing hand info + minor chip damage is lower-value than more doom density once ahead) starves the whole ledger family permanently with no counterplay available to Abjuration.

### Every ledger spend and its alternative cost
- **Sealed Verdict, 1 cast, 1 success** (R8T64, cancelled Far Sight, spent 6 bank). Alternative: Phase Shift was also armed at that moment and could have done the same job for free (no bank cost) — the only reason to prefer Verdict here was to conserve Phase Shift for Foreclosure/Unbind later that round. Given Runic Seal + Phase Shift + Counterbind already exist as *free* (no ongoing resource cost beyond components) cancels, Sealed Verdict's actual marginal value is: a *fourth* denial action in a round where all three free tools are already spoken for. That happened exactly zero times this game — every Verdict cast could, in principle, have been done by an already-armed free reaction instead. Its real selling point (per this one test) is flexibility/redundancy, not a strictly new capability.
- **Warding Tithe: never cast.** Correctly identified per the brief's warning — with Fortify (free, S cost, +2 ward, removes a burn marker) and Arcane Shell (free, S cost, +1 ward +2-card draw-on-pop) both sitting in the same 1-cast/turn budget, Tithe (spend up to 4 bank for a ward that size) is strictly worse: it costs the same S-cost cast slot AND drains the bank that Verdict/Rune need, for a ward that Fortify already produces for free. There was never a round where casting Tithe over Fortify/Shell would have been correct.
- **Restoring Rune: never cast — the actual failure point.** Prepped R10 specifically because HP had cratered to 3 and this is exactly the emergency the card should answer. Bank was 0 at that moment (spent on the one Verdict use two rounds earlier, no Foretell resolved since) and never recovered. This is the clearest evidence the family can't be relied on for emergency healing: the moments you most need Restoring Rune (HP critical) are exactly the moments a doom-focused Divination has usually already stopped feeding the bank, because it's racing to close the game rather than chip with Foretell.

### Anomalies
- **Deck-reshuffle costs 2 HP directly** (R9T71→T73: "P0 reshuffles discard into deck (#1) — exhaustion 2 / P0 takes 2 damage"). This is a new, sharp finding — sustained denial spending (funding Runic Seal/Phase Shift/Counterbind/Sealed Verdict every round) burns through the 40-card resource deck fast enough to force a reshuffle by R9, and the reshuffle itself is punished with direct damage. This stacked directly with a queued Foreclosure that same turn (4+2=6 damage in two back-to-back ticks) and was the proximate cause of the HP9→3 collapse that made Entropy lethal two rounds later. Worth flagging as a standalone balance concern independent of the ledger family.
- **Divination adapting its own prep list mid-game to starve the counter-strategy** (swapping Foretell out for Entropy at R10 prep) is either sophisticated greedy-bot play or coincidence, but the effect is the same either way: it's a hard counter to the entire ledger family that requires no special targeting, just normal deck optimization toward doom density.
- Sealed Vault (Div gambit, R6) reshuffled their deck from a low count back to full — same pattern noted in m24, removes any decking-out pressure on Divination's side while Abjuration has no equivalent tool.

### Is the ledger family dead weight vs Divination? Mostly yes, with one caveat
Two of three cards (Warding Tithe, Restoring Rune) never found a correct cast in a full 10-round game — Tithe because free alternatives dominate it, Restoring Rune because the bank was empty at every moment it would have mattered. Sealed Verdict justified its slot once, doing real work (a clean doom cancel) — but it's redundant with tools Abjuration already has whenever the bank happens to be funded, and dead whenever it isn't, which is most of the game against a school that can simply stop casting its one direct-damage spell. The family doesn't fix the 0-3 problem from m22-m24 (still lost, and lost worse in some sense — dead at -4 rather than grinding to 0) because it doesn't address the actual bottleneck identified there: Divination can fund 2-3 threats per round and Abjuration's proactive/reactive denial toolkit (Runic Seal + Phase Shift + Counterbind, all free) can only stop one or two. Adding a bank-gated fourth answer that's frequently uncharged doesn't move that arithmetic. Recommend: if this family is meant to matter here, it needs a bank source that doesn't depend on the opponent choosing to cast direct-damage spells — e.g., let doom damage itself feed the bank at a reduced rate (even 25%), since that's the one damage type Divination can't opt out of dealing.
