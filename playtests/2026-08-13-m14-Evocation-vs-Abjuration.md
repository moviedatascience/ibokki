## Analysis

PILOTED RE-RUN (post-fix): Evocation (me, P0) vs greedy Abjuration bot, seed 3202 — identical seed to m12, which the pilot dominated (16 HP vs opponent's 3 HP) and then LOST in round 10 to a single 25-damage Reckoning [ABJ-032] hit. RESULT THIS GAME: WIN, round 9, P0 19 HP — P1 0 HP. Opponent was killed one full round before Reckoning becomes available (unlocks at level 10 / round 10), so the swingy one-shot never got a chance to fire.

== RESULT ==
P0 (Evocation) wins by HP, round 9. Hex Bolt's final 3 damage brought P1 from 3 to 0. P0 ended at 19 HP — never seriously threatened on life total the entire game (lowest point was 19, reached via self-inflicted costs: Battle Trance -2, exhaustion -2, Ward Pulse chip -1 x3, Reflective Ward reflect -1 x1, Lightning Bolt self -1 x2, Hex Bolt self -1 x1, Volatile Charge self -1 x1 — all told P0 took ~11 damage across 9 rounds, essentially zero organic pressure from the bot's spells).

== HOW IT ACTUALLY HAPPENED ==
Followed a stricter version of the m11/m12 doctrine: refused ALL casts that would net zero face damage (not just the big ones) — held Crackle/VC/Backdraft any time a ward or Stone Stance was up unless there was a secondary reason (discard denial with their hand non-empty, or wall-clearing when a wall was about to grow further). Used Dispelling Powder proactively on a 5-HP ward the moment it was drawn rather than saving it, since destroying a ward is bank-free regardless of size or timing. Swapped Volatile Charge for Hex Bolt (unreactable) as the very first L5 lineup change, before Absorb ever got to punish anything — never once fed a reactable big spell into an armed or suspected-armed Absorb/Counterbind slot; every point of face damage came from either unreactable Hex Bolt casts, Backdraft fired only when no ward was up, or Lightning Bolt/Detonate fired into a fully clean, unarmed board. R6 alpha: hoarded hand to 10 cards (naturally, via cap-discards) and Detonated the instant the round started, before any reaction could be armed — 13 clean face (28→15). R8 alpha (the decisive round): opponent swapped Stone Stance out for Break Form that round, leaving zero armed reactions at turn 1 — combined Battle Trance (+3) with Lightning Bolt into their two 3-HP wards, which overflowed sequentially through both wards and still spilled 1 net to face (after Reflective Ward's flat self-damage clause hit me back), then followed up same-round with a clean Hex Bolt (3) and a Searing Word (1) that Absorb didn't even bother contesting — opponent went from 13 HP to 8 HP in a single round with zero reactions fired against me. R9: two more clean hits (Lightning Bolt+Chalk for 5, then Hex Bolt for the kill) closed it out at 3 HP before Reckoning ever became preppable.

== SCORECARD ==
Face damage to P1 (30→0 over the game): Backdraft 2+2=4, Detonate 13, Lightning Bolt 1 (R8, overflow spillover after two wards) + 5 (R9, chalked) = 6, Hex Bolt 3+3=6, Searing Word 1+1=2, exhaustion tick 2, Volatile Charge 3 (R2). Sum ≈ 4+13+6+6+2+2+3 = 36 against a 30-HP-plus-healed pool (P1 healed 5 via Second Wind in R5, so effective total damage taken ≈35, consistent with the tracked 30→0 plus the mid-game heal).
Bank fed (traceable, all minor): R1 Crackle 3 (wall-clear, no spillover), R2 Searing Word 1, R3 nothing (Dispelling Powder cleared for free), R4 Burning Hands 1 (ward) + burn-tick 1 (ward, see engine notes), R7/R8 essentially nothing (walls were either cleared via Detonate/Lightning Bolt overflow with spillover, or Reflective Ward's self-damage clause hit ME not them). Estimated total lifetime-prevented-damage banked against P1 this game: roughly 6-8 points, an order of magnitude below m12's ~50. Never got the chance to matter since the kill landed at R9, one round before Reckoning unlocks.
Their reactions: Absorb was armed (SS-funded) on at least 3 separate occasions (R8 T83, R9 T86/T88) and never fired once — every spell I cast while it was up was either unreactable (Hex Bolt) or low-enough-value (Searing Word's 1 dmg) that the bot declined to spend it. Reflective Ward fired its self-damage clause once (1 dmg to me) when its ward absorbed part of a Lightning Bolt. Counterbind, Interrupt, Phase Shift were all prepared at various points but never funded/fired — no opportunity, since P0 never gave them a target worth cancelling.

== COMPARISON TO m12 (same seed) ==
m12 and m14 open identically through R1-R2 (bot's deterministic opening: Fortify→Bulwark Shard, Stone Stance at L3, Absorb+Reflective Ward swap-in at L5 — all matched exactly, confirming the seed drives P1's RNG draws/choices deterministically regardless of P0's play). The decisive divergence: (1) m12 cast Crackle/VC into full 4-HP wards for zero face damage multiple times in R1-R2, feeding the bank for nothing; m14 refused those same casts. (2) m12 kept Volatile Charge until L5 then swapped to Fireball (reactable, 5 dmg); m14 swapped straight to Hex Bolt (unreactable, 3 dmg) at the same L5 juncture, sacrificing 2 raw damage per cast in exchange for total Absorb-immunity from that point forward. (3) m12 cast Fireball+Battle Trance and then Wrath of the Mage directly into an armed/suspected Absorb (R7, R9), losing the entire value of both casts and feeding Reckoning's bank ~12 points from full pre-mitigation prevention; m14 never made that mistake — every Trance/Chalk combo this game went into either an unreactable spell or a confirmed-clean board. (4) m12 needed until R10 T2 to bring P1 to 3 HP, one round too slow; m14 reached the same 3-HP mark by R9 T88 (a full round earlier) because Hex Bolt/Lightning Bolt landed damage in windows m12 spent passing or feeding walls. The net effect: same opening, same opponent RNG, but P0's discipline about WHEN and WHAT to cast — not a different card pool — was the entire difference between a 25-damage death and a clean round-9 kill.

== VERDICT ON THE EDGE ==
This result does not overturn the pilot-gap doctrine's core lesson (Reckoning as a designed swing mechanic is still a genuine problem for any line that reaches R10 with meaningful lifetime-prevented-damage banked) — it instead validates the doctrine's prescribed counter-play: refuse-to-feed + kill-before-R10 is achievable at genuine best play on this exact seed, converting what looked like a coinflip (m12: dominant-position loss) into a clean, comfortable win with 19 HP to spare and zero rounds of real life-total pressure from the bot. Combined with m11 (decisive Evo win, same doctrine) and the earlier piloted series, the throughline across this seed family is that Evocation's damage race is comfortably ahead of Abjuration's wall/reaction kit at good-to-best play — the ONLY way Abjuration wins is if Evocation feeds Reckoning's bank and/or fails to close before R10. That is a balance statement about Reckoning's design (still needs a visible bank counter / cap / lower conversion — unchanged recommendation from m12), not about the school matchup itself, which reads Evocation-favored on this seed at disciplined play.

== PRICING NOTES ==
- Hex Bolt [EVO-012] (VV, unreactable): performed exactly as doctrine predicted — the load-bearing tool of the win. Every cast landed full value, Absorb never got to touch it, and its mere presence in the lineup appears to have suppressed the bot from ever firing Absorb at all (it may be timing Absorb only for its highest-EV target and finding none once Hex Bolt is in the mix — worth a dedicated bot-behavior check).
- Lightning Bolt [EVO-018] (VV, "cannot be reduced below 1"): strong pick for wall-clearing — unlike Hex Bolt (which exactly-kills a matching-size ward with 0 spillover) its higher base (4) plus Trance/Chalk consistently overkills single wards for guaranteed spillover. NEW ENGINE FINDING: overkill damage against multiple simultaneous wards carries through sequentially (R8: 7 damage split 3+3+1 across two 3-HP wards then face) rather than stopping at the first ward — worth confirming this is intended (it reads correct/expected, matches "wards form a chain the attack punches through").
- Reflective Ward [ABJ-012] (SS, "When this Ward prevents damage, deal 1 damage to your opponent"): confirmed the clause is a FLAT 1, not proportional to damage prevented — triggered once for exactly 1 regardless of how much of a 7-point Lightning Bolt it actually absorbed. Minor but real chip-back tool for Abjuration that a pure-refusal doctrine can't fully dodge (it fires passively whenever its ward blocks anything, including overflow spillover situations).
- Dispelling Powder [GAM-012] (free Gambit, "Destroy target Ward or ongoing effect"): used proactively the moment it was drawn (on a 5-HP ward, R3) rather than hoarded per m12's pattern — no downside observed to using it immediately; the only real ward-persistence risk (a bigger wall later) is fully mitigated since destroying costs nothing regardless of ward size. Also newly confirmed it can target "ongoing effects" (i.e. Stone Stance itself), an option not tested this game but worth remembering — may be an even better target than a ward on rounds where stance is blanking a big planned turn.
- Reckoning [ABJ-032] (SS, L3-tagged but effectively level-10-gated by prep availability): never got prepared this game (P1 was only L9 when the game ended) — the "kill by end of R9" deadline is confirmed as a hard, correct cutoff: P1's own prep choices at R9's "done preparing" showed no attempt to rush it in, consistent with it being genuinely unavailable before L10.
- Burning Hands [EVO-003] (V, 1 dmg + Burn marker): RETESTED per assignment brief. Confirmed m11's finding still holds post-fix: the burn tick at the start of P1's turn was absorbed by their ward exactly like direct damage ("P1 burns for 1 / P1 ward destroyed" — no HP loss, only ward HP consumed). Burn markers themselves get placed even through an active ward (the placement effect isn't itself "damage"), but the tick payout when it fires IS checked against wards/mitigation like any other damage instance. This contradicts the design-doc framing that burn should bypass wards; unaffected by today's reaction-legality fix (as expected, since that fix only touched reaction-vs-reaction/spell targeting legality, not damage-type routing). One incidental upside found: a burn tick can passively whittle a small ward for free (no cast slot, no fuel) even though it can't reach face through one.

4. As in m12, no adaptation to the refusal pattern — P1 kept building walls/stance turn after turn even during long stretches where P0 was visibly not casting into them, never pivoting to pressure P0's life total directly (P0 ended at 19/30, essentially undamaged by design).

== ENGINE OBSERVATIONS ==
1. POST-FIX REACTION LEGALITY: no anomalies observed. Backdraft (spell-cast-triggered reaction) was fired multiple times against P1's spell casts (Fortify, Stone Stance) and never against a P1 Reaction (none were offered as targets, consistent with the fix). This matters a lot for the refuse-to-feed doctrine: there's no "free" ward decay to wait out, only Dispelling Powder or overkill-through-spillover clears one.
3. Overkill damage against multiple simultaneous wards chains through them sequentially before spilling to face (see Pricing Notes, Lightning Bolt entry) — new finding, not observed in m11/m12/m13.
4. Reflective Ward's damage-back clause is flat-1-per-prevention-event, not proportional (see Pricing Notes).
5. Burn-tick-vs-ward interaction reconfirmed unchanged post-fix (see Pricing Notes, Burning Hands entry).
6. Deck-exhaustion mechanic reconfirmed: both sides hit deck-0 in R7 simultaneously, triggering a reshuffle + exhaustion-tier-2 damage (2 to each side) — matches m12's finding, this is a real secondary clock independent of Reckoning.
7. One cast-slot-consuming spell per turn-visit, gambits/items free of that limit, hand-cap-10 discards trigger end-of-turn once hand exceeds cap rather than only at round boundaries — all reconfirmed unchanged from m12.
