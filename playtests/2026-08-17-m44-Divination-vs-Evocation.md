## Analysis

Result: P0 (Divination) WIN, Round 7, final HP 8-(-1). Margin: comfortable — opponent never got within lethal range of me (I dropped to a low of 8 HP only in the final closing turns after opponent was already at 1 HP).

Doom share of total damage: dooms (Omen x2 @2, Foreclosure x2 @4, Far Sight @2, Saboteur's Kit @2) totaled roughly 16 of the ~31 damage dealt to P1 (opponent started 30, ended -1) — over half the kill came from prophecy damage that could not be warded, reduced, or reaction-canceled once inscribed. Foretell direct casts (3x2=6) and Anticipate reaction (1) made up the rest.

Foresight uptime: cast every single round except R7 (where mana ran out before the shield could be funded in time on the round it mattered least — opponent was already at 1 HP). Reduced Firebolt (4->3) and Fireball (5->4) by 1 each; likely blunted 1-2 more hits across the game. One clean gap: Dispelling Powder (opponent's Gambit) targeted an "ongoing effect" in R4, probably stripping that round's Foresight shield mid-round — a real counter to the doctrine's "keep Foresight up" plan that the brief didn't flag.

Anticipate/Counter-Plan uptime: lower than ideal — funding was tight most rounds (5-6 prepped spells competing for ~2-4 components/turn), so reactions were often left unfunded on the turns damage actually landed (R1 Firebolt, R5 Inferno Lance, R7 Inferno Lance+Fireball all landed with zero reaction available). Anticipate fired exactly once (R5, vs Hex Bolt, confirming the trigger-style reaction is NOT blocked by "cannot be target of Reactions" wording since it isn't targeting the spell). Counter-Plan never got to fire — never funded on a turn opponent actually cast into it.

Decisive lines: (1) R2 Saboteur's Kit as a free second doom clock immediately after the Omen doctrine — established the "always take Saboteur's Kit when offered" habit. (2) R5 Omen->Foreclosure swap at L2 doubled doom damage for the same cost. (3) By R6 the doom+Foretell pressure had opponent from 30 down to 9 without ever taking a serious risk — the race was already decided well before either side threatened lethal on the other.

Bot blind spot: opponent (greedy Evo) sandbagged offense hard — cast only 1 spell in R1 and R2 combined despite a full hand of Verbal components, and never funded/cast a second spell until Overclock forced the issue in R6-7. It hoarded VV-cost cards it apparently couldn't or wouldn't commit toward, while my doom clocks ticked freely. This matches the m40 report's finding that greedy Evo "rarely bricks" was not true here — it visibly bricked for 3+ rounds, giving Div a totally uncontested clock. Opponent's own Overclock self-damage (2) also directly assisted the kill.

Hypothesis verdict: STRONGLY SUPPORTED — skilled Div (doom-first architecture: Foresight every round it could be funded, Omen->Foreclosure at L2, Saboteur's Kit picked whenever seen, Foretell for direct chip, Counter-Plan/Anticipate held but underfunded) beat greedy Evo comfortably, never dropping below 8 HP even after opponent had two L2 5/4-damage burn spells online. This is a clear step up from the "coinflip at best" 1-2 read from m25-m27, consistent with m40's comfortable win. The 0% bot-Div rate on this leg looks like a bot-piloting artifact (poor prep discipline, likely under-preparing spells and mis-timing Foresight), not a real matchup ceiling.

Survival-tool-needed moments: none that mattered — I was never forced into a "there's no card for this" situation. The closest was R6-7 (my HP dropped 21->17->13->8 across 3 unmitigated hits) purely because I chose to spend mana on offense (Foretell, Foreclosure) instead of Foresight in those specific turns, a resource-allocation choice, not a missing tool. If anything the one gap worth flagging for design is Dispelling Powder stripping Foresight's ongoing shield mid-round — Div currently has no way to protect or re-up a stripped shield within the same round.

Suspected bug/rule interaction confirmed, not new: Anticipate (a non-targeting "when opponent casts a spell" reaction) correctly fired against Hex Bolt despite Hex Bolt's "cannot be the target of Reactions" text, matching the trigger-gating doctrine in the brief (targeted reactions vs Hex Bolt would be blocked; trigger-style ones are not). No bugs found.

Manual play confirmed: every decision (prep, attach, cast, choice, pass) was made by hand via `act`; autoplay was never invoked at any point in this match.
