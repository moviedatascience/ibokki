## Analysis

### Verdict: ledger family creates real optionality; doctrine held; Reckoning is now sharper AND riskier

**Result:** P0 (Abjuration) wins by HP, round 11. Final HP: P0 16, P1 -10 (Reckoning overkill by 10 with a single L3 cast). Margin was comfortable in the end but the game passed through a genuinely dangerous stretch (R10-R11, P0 dropped from 35 HP to a low of 16 HP across one huge risk/reward exchange plus one unabsorbed Fireball).

Total: 12 prevented-damage points converted to 12 HP of ward across three Warding Tithe casts. Every one of those 12 points is 12 points Reckoning would otherwise have converted to ~6 extra damage (each point spent costs Reckoning ceil(1/2) ≈ 0.5 damage at the margin). Concretely: without those three spends, the bank feeding the final Reckoning would have been ~12 higher, i.e. Reckoning would have hit for ~27 instead of 21 — comfortably lethal either way this game, but in a closer game that 6-damage difference is exactly the kind of margin m21's 1-HP finish was made of.

### Did optionality work?

Yes — this was the core question and the answer is a clean yes. Every Warding Tithe cast was a real decision with a real opportunity cost, not a rote button-press:
- Early (R7 T73, R9): wards had been ground down by repeated Fireball/Inferno Lance hits and a Dispelling Powder pop; converting bank to immediate ward HP was the right call because I didn't yet have Reckoning online (pre-L10) and a broken defense loses games regardless of banked math.
- Mid-late (R9-R10, once Reckoning was prepped at L10): I explicitly held the bank rather than spending, reasoning through the tradeoff live (recorded via `note`) — "bank is 5... holding it rather than spending... Reckoning from L10 is ceil(bank/2) repeatable damage." This is exactly the spend-vs-hold tension the assignment asked me to find, and it was legible turn to turn, not a foregone conclusion.
- Sealed Verdict sat loaded the entire game and was never worth firing: every incoming spell I checked against the ward+Stone-Stance stack turned out to be fully absorbed for free, so paying 6 bank to cancel it outright was strictly worse than doing nothing. That's a real, repeatable finding, not a fluke of this seed — see below.

### Was the soak-refill loop degenerate or fair?

Fair, with a caveat. Wards soaking damage refilled the bank at a 1:1 rate every single hit (confirmed against the engine: `state-ops.ts` ward-soak path unconditionally credits `damagePreventedTotal`), and Stone Stance's flat -2 stacked on top of that as a second, layered prevention source before the ward ever took the hit. That means a ward-plus-Stone-Stance board doesn't just survive Evocation's burn — it profits from tanking it, and the profit compounds turn over turn (my bank reached the 40s by round 11 purely from six or seven Evocation casts landing on a stone-stanced ward-wall). That's the "attrition engine" the brief warned about, and it is real: rounds 1-9 were a long grind where nothing meaningful happened to either side's HP.

But it isn't degenerate, because (a) it costs real card slots and real turns to keep Stone Stance + Fortify + Ward Pulse online every round, (b) the opponent has direct counterplay in the form of Dispelling Powder (popped my 8-HP ward outright, no bank refund for me on that loss since the ward was simply destroyed by a non-damage effect) and Combust/Combustive Sigil (which punish the *other* half of the loop — reactions — hard enough that I never once found it correct to fire Echo Shield or Sealed Verdict all game), and (c) it doesn't win the game by itself — it only wins if you eventually convert the bank via Reckoning, which is a separate, level-gated, and (see below) newly risky decision.

### Sealed Verdict's effect on reaction economy

None, functionally — and that's itself the finding. Across roughly seven incoming Evocation spells I evaluated Echo Shield and Sealed Verdict against, the ward-plus-Stone-Stance stack was sufficient to fully absorb the net damage every single time (the only exceptions were the one turn Stone Stance wasn't up yet, and Hex Bolt which is explicitly reaction-proof). Doctrine says "never react while Combust is armed"; this match shows a second, independent reason to hold reactions even when Combust *isn't* confirmed armed: once your wards are healthy, a reaction's marginal prevention is usually redundant with what the ward would have eaten for free, so firing it only exposes you to a punish reaction for zero gain. Sealed Verdict's "only ONE attached card" restriction (vs Phase Shift's two) never came up as a binding constraint — the card was never worth casting at all in this game, purely on the free-absorption math, independent of its attach-count design.

### Reckoning interaction with Final Riposte — genuine anomaly, worth flagging to design

The single biggest event of the game: R10 T99, I cast Reckoning at bank ~26 for 13 damage to P1 — and P1 had Final Riposte armed (a level-11 reaction: "when a spell deals damage to you, deal that damage doubled to your opponent"). It fired, and Reckoning's *own* 13 damage to P1 got doubled and reflected back at me as 26 raw damage (mitigated by my own Stone Stance + wards down to 16 taken). That's a 35→19 HP swing off one spell. This was not in the pre-ledger-family reference doctrine (m19-m21) because Reckoning wasn't being played against decks holding Final Riposte at that scale before, or the ledger cards hadn't pushed banks this high.

Two follow-on findings for design:
1. **Reckoning is a genuine bank-conversion risk, not a free lunch**, once the opponent has any "reflect the actual damage dealt" reaction available (Final Riposte, possibly others). A big bank is now a double-edged sword: great if unanswered, catastrophic if reflected. This is a *good* tension to have (mirrors the ledger-family's spend/hold theme at the top of the curve) but it should be intentional, not incidental — worth confirming Final Riposte's interaction with Reckoning was designed-for rather than an emergent combo.
2. **A lethal Reckoning skips the reflect entirely** (confirmed in `packages/engine/src/stack.ts`: the reflect-onto-caster branch is gated on `state.phase !== "gameover"`), which is what let the R11 rematch (bank ~42, Reckoning for 21 vs P1's 11 HP) close the game safely — the moment the killing blow lands, Final Riposte's reflection never resolves. This is a legitimate, findable line ("if your Reckoning is lethal, cast it — the reflect can't punish a dead opponent") but it's a fairly deep interaction to expect players to find live; flagging it as a design note rather than a bug.

### Anomalies

- **Opponent (greedy bot) passivity, rounds 1-5**: P1 built up multi-component spells in prep slots across several rounds and never cast them, then swapped them out for different cards at round boundaries — wasting all the attached fuel every time (attachments don't persist across rounds per the ruleset). This produced a ~5-round dead stretch where my bank stayed at exactly 0 (nothing was ever cast at me to prevent). This is very likely a known greedy-bot planning quirk rather than a ledger-family effect, but it materially shaped this game's pacing — the "long attrition engine" only really started at R6 once the bot started actually casting its prepared spells.
- **Deck exhaustion / reshuffle damage**: both players independently hit deck-0 and took unpreventable "exhaustion" damage on reshuffle (2 damage each, first occurrence) — a real mechanic that interacted with nothing in the ledger family but is worth noting as a resource pressure that showed up mid-game (R7) purely from the sheer number of attach/detach/reprep cycles this deck style generates.
- **Fortify double-cast attempt (R6 T58)**: tried to recast Fortify twice in the same round on two different turns; the engine correctly blocked the second cast (a prepared spell may be cast once per ROUND, not once per turn) — not a bug, just confirms the granularity for future note-taking.
- **Warding Tithe always spent the max (4)**: never had a reason to spend less than the cap in this game — bank was always comfortably ≥4 by the time I chose to fire it. Never got to observe a genuine "spend partial" decision; that would need either an earlier cast (before bank exceeds 4) or a smaller emergency top-up. Worth another data point in a future game.
