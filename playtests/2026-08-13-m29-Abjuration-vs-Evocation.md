### Verdict's reaction economy

Sealed Verdict changed my reaction calculus in a real way: it's a full, unconditional cancel for one card (vs. Phase Shift's two), and — critically — it competes with Echo Shield/Grounding for "do I react at all," not just for components. I held it through a whiffing Searing Word in R5 (not worth 6 bank on a trivial 1-dmg spell with nothing in hand to discard) and fired it in R13 against a buffed Fireball (5+1 dmg) specifically because letting it resolve would have cost real ward HP versus spending bank I could afford. The single successful cancel fully removed the spell from the stack with no side effects (no discard, no partial resolution) and no Combust-style penalty since Combust wasn't armed that round.

**Combust interaction (doctrine confirmed, extended):** with Combust armed in R6, I avoided all reactions and instead used the turn's cast slot to convert the bank into a Ward via Warding Tithe. This is a genuinely new tactical line the ledger family opens up: Wards absorb damage passively (no action, no Combust trigger), so under reaction-punish pressure, Warding Tithe becomes strictly better than Echo Shield/Sealed Verdict — the ledger spend-cards let you sidestep a reaction tax that the pre-existing toolkit couldn't.

### Is the soak-refill loop degenerate or fair counterplay?

Fair, with real counters, confirmed by reading the implementation directly (not just observed behavior):

- `dealDamageToPlayer` (state-ops.ts) credits `damagePreventedTotal` for BOTH the ongoing-reduction portion (Stone Stance) AND ward-soak absorption, so a Ward built by Tithe that fully blocks a hit refunds its cost precisely as the brief predicted — sometimes more (if it absorbs more than it cost).
- BUT two purpose-built Evocation answers bypass this entirely: Dispelling Powder (destroy one Ward, seen 3x) and Chain Lightning's flat 2-dmg-to-every-Ward sweep (seen 2x) both route through `dealDamageToWard`/`damageAllWards`, which never touch `damagePreventedTotal`. These strip Ward HP for free — genuine hard counters to a wide, shallow ward stack. Chain Lightning in particular punished my "many small 4-HP wards" pattern hard: one cast wiped 4 of 6 wards (15→4 HP) via the flat sweep, while a single big Ward Eternal (10 HP) would have shrugged it off far better. This is a real design lever: the ledger loop rewards consolidating into fewer, bigger wards against a Chain-Lightning-style meta, not spreading into many small ones.
- The loop is also NOT infinite/free: `spendPrevented(max)` (context.ts) hard-caps every ledger card's draw at its own printed cap AND the actual bank, so Tithe/Rune/Verdict can only ever take what's there.

### Did optionality actually work? Mixed — one real design gap found

The brief asked whether the ledger family gives skilled play "real choices between spending and holding." Two levels of answer:

1. **Macro-level choice: yes, and it's the most interesting thing about this family.** Reckoning reads the bank but (confirmed via `packages/engine/src/effects/abjuration.ts`) never calls `spendPrevented` — it's non-consuming and repeatable, while Tithe/Verdict/Rune all withdraw principal via `spendPrevented`. That makes the real strategic question "grow-and-preserve the bank for a bigger/more frequent Reckoning tax, or cash out now for a concrete Ward/cancel/heal" — a genuine tension that isn't visible from the card text alone (nothing on any of the four cards states that Reckoning doesn't spend the bank; I only found this by reading the source). This asymmetry is the single most important thing to surface to the design — it's arguably the game's actual answer to "does the ledger family create real choices."

2. **Micro-level "spend up to N" choice: no — there is none.** `spendPrevented(max)` always withdraws `min(max, bank)`, with zero player input. Every Tithe cast either produced exactly the amount I predicted from the printed cap or the actual (lower) bank — never a partial, deliberately-smaller draw. The "spend up to" phrasing on all three cards implies a choice that isn't implemented; the actual choice is entirely at the macro level (which spend-card, and when), never within a single cast.

3. **A real, punishing consequence of over-withdrawing, discovered live:** by R17 I had cast Reckoning three times, Restoring Rune five times, Tithe roughly a dozen times, Sealed Verdict once, and Collapse the Veil once (SSSS, a big single withdrawal) — and in R16-17 I also chose several times to take raw damage instead of reacting, since I was HP-safe. That stopped feeding the bank at exactly the moment heavy spending was draining it. Result: Reckoning resolved for **zero damage** in R17 (bank was empty), and Warding Tithe became temporarily uncastable (its LEDGER_MIN of 1, from `cardFlags.ts`, wasn't met). This is exactly the richness the brief is asking about, but it only shows up under sustained, aggressive spending — bot-level play that casts Tithe every round without variation would likely never hit it, which may explain why the m19-21-style bot benchmarks read the loop as a smooth "attrition engine" rather than a bank you can actually break.

### Anomalies / findings for the design log

- **No player choice on ledger-card amount spent.** `spendPrevented` always maxes out (capped by both the card's own cap and the bank). If "up to N" is meant to convey agency, either the UI needs a real amount-picker, or the card text should just say "spend N" / drop "up to."
- **Reckoning is non-consuming and repeatable** (doesn't call `spendPrevented`) — this is the load-bearing asymmetry that makes the whole family interesting, and it is not evident from any card's printed text.
- **Reckoning can resolve for 0 damage** if the bank is empty — no floor, no minimum. Worth a design look: is a whiffing L3 spell (2 components, a full cast slot) intended, or should Reckoning have its own LEDGER_MIN like the other three?
- **Direct ward-damage effects (Dispelling Powder, Chain Lightning) never credit the bank** — confirmed in code, this is the load-bearing counterplay that keeps the soak-refill loop from being degenerate. Worth documenting explicitly as intentional anti-Abjuration tech, since it's easy to mistake for a missed interaction.
- **No hard HP cap** — Restoring Rune pushed HP from 28 to 31, past the starting 30. Overheal is real and not wasted; my own note about "capping at 30" from an earlier heal (R6, 27→30 exactly) was a coincidence, not a rule — corrected mid-transcript.
- **Two-component-cap trap for SSSS costs:** Collapse the Veil / any 4-pip cost can ONLY be funded by exactly two same-symbol duals (e.g. 2× CMP-SS); a single-pip component wastes an attachment slot since no combination of one single + one dual reaches 4. Cost me a couple of turns of fumbling before I worked this out live — not a bug, but a rough edge worth a possible in-UI hint.
- **Unstoppable ≠ Unpreventable:** Unstoppable Bolt blocked Sealed Verdict (can't be cancelled by a Reaction) but still soaked into my Ward normally — confirmed the flag only blocks reaction-based cancel/redirect/reduce, not the base damage pipeline. Matches the Apocalypse precedent in cardFlags.ts.
- **Chain Lightning's ward-sweep punishes wide/shallow stacks disproportionately** — a real reason to occasionally consolidate (e.g. via Fortify/Bulwark Shard onto one Ward, or Ward Eternal) rather than always taking Tithe's new-Ward-per-cast default.
- **Deck-exhaustion penalty escalates and is symmetric** (both sides took 2, then 4, then 6 damage on successive reshuffles) — decided this game's final blow (opponent's own reshuffle killed them at 1 HP) rather than anything I cast. Worth noting since it means a long ledger-family attrition game can be decided by a mechanic unrelated to the ledger at all.

### Verdict

Skilled play makes real, load-bearing decisions in this family — but the richest one (Reckoning-preserve vs. ledger-cash-out) is invisible on the cards themselves and only surfaces from either reading the code or playing long enough to break the bank. The intra-card "spend up to N" choice promised by the text does not exist in the current implementation. The soak-refill loop is NOT degenerate: Dispelling Powder and Chain Lightning are real, already-shipped counters that bypass the bank entirely, and Chain Lightning specifically punishes the "always take a new small Ward" default that Tithe encourages. Recommend either implementing a genuine partial-spend choice, or dropping "up to" from the three cards' text to match what they actually do; and flagging Reckoning's non-consuming behavior as either the intended headline feature (in which case it deserves a rules note) or an oversight (in which case a LEDGER_MIN a la the other three is worth considering).
