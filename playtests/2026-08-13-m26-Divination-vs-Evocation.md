## Analysis

RESULT: Divination (piloted) def. greedy Evocation, round 7, final HP 1–(-2). Seed 6302. Game 2 of the m26-series NEW-DECK calibration (resource decks redesigned same day: 15 primary basics, 8 same-symbol duals, demand-split crosses — Div got 6 VM + 2 SM + 8 MM duals; Evo got 8 VV + 6 VM). Bot-level baseline for this edge: 100% Evo, ~5.6 rounds. This game ran to round 7 and came down to the last HP on the board — confirms the brief's expectation of "the hardest version of this race yet."

DOES THE DOOM-CLOCK LINE SURVIVE THE NEW DECKS — yes, largely intact, with two new counter-tech wrinkles:
- Omen R1-T1: confirmed, cast turn one exactly as doctrine specifies, funded in a single MM card (the new-deck fix working as intended — no more assembling MM from two separate M draws).
- Foretell every round: confirmed literally — cast in every round 1 through 6 (6/6), never skipped. It was the primary damage engine (12 dmg across 6 casts) and repeatedly the correct choice over delayed doom when the race was close.
- Anticipate every window: mostly held — 4 successful fires (R1, R3, R4, R5) for 4 dmg + 4 free draws, but it did NOT fire in R2 (opponent's first cast landed before I could arm it — the documented "cannot fire on their first cast of a round when they have first seat" constraint, confirmed again on the new decks) and was deliberately WITHHELD in R6 by pilot decision (see Combust, below) — this is new information the old briefs didn't have to weigh.
- Premonition first on 3-slot rounds: confirmed on R3 (the only 3-slot round it got to run in) — dug 3 cards including Saboteur's Kit [GAM-019], the free second doom clock, exactly matching the doctrine's expected payoff.
- Foreclosure timed to fire at their turn-start for the exact kill: confirmed twice. First copy (R5) landed the round-5 kill shot down to HP2 non-lethally because Foretell/Anticipate had already chipped them there first; the second copy (R6, re-cast after the first resolved) delivered the actual game-ending blow, carrying across the R6→R7 round boundary and firing at the start of P1's R7 turn — BEFORE they could act — for the win. This is the clearest confirmation yet of "an end-of-round doom is a pre-loaded next-round opener."
- Foresight underrated defense: confirmed valuable (Spark 2→1, Concussive Blast components in R4/R5) but also shown to have a hard counter this game — see Dispelling Powder below.
- Near-zero wasted M: NOT achieved — see the round-boundary anomaly below, a real self-inflicted cost this game that the doctrine phrase now needs to account for explicitly.

NEW COUNTER-TECH FROM THE REDESIGNED EVO KIT (not in the m1-4/m16-18 briefs, both discovered R5-R6):
1. **Dispelling Powder [GAM-012]** (Neutral Gambit, free): "Destroy target Ward or ongoing effect." Opponent used it on my Foresight's active -1-damage-per-spell effect immediately before casting Burning Hands at R5-T30, stripping the mitigation I'd paid a full cast for. This is a direct, cheap answer to Foresight and the first time it's been observed on this line.
2. **Combust [EVO-016]** (Evocation Reaction, VV, swapped in at R6 prep): "When your opponent plays a Reaction, deal 4 damage to them. The Reaction still resolves." This is a hard counter to Anticipate — since Anticipate is my only Reaction, arming it at low HP risks eating 4 unavoidable damage on top of whatever they were already casting. At HP5 and HP1 in R6 I deliberately declined to arm Anticipate specifically because of this threat, which cost me the reactive 1-dmg/card-draw value in the exact rounds I most wanted it. This is a real strategic tax the new Evo loadout can apply to the doom-clock pilot's standard line and should be logged as a counter-play the design now supports.

ANOMALY — round-boundary attachment loss (self-inflicted, cost ~3 cards): at the end of R1 I pre-attached components to spells for "next round's cast" (CMP-MM on Omen, CMP-M on Foretell, CMP-SM on Premonition) without casting them, assuming attachments would persist into R2. They did not — round-end wiped all uncast attachments with no salvage, while the opponent explicitly detached their own unused attachments before passing to reclaim them to hand. Net: I lost 3 cards of resources (4 pips) that a one-line habit (detach before round end, or don't pre-fund across a round boundary) would have prevented. This is exactly what "near-zero wasted M is the bar" is warning against, but the doctrine phrasing didn't previously spell out the mechanism — worth codifying explicitly: **funded-but-uncast attachments are lost at round end; only HAND cards carry over.** I self-corrected from R2 onward (held cards in hand instead of pre-attaching across a round boundary) and this did not recur.

EVO'S OBSERVED CLOCK SPEED vs old references: markedly faster once L2 unlocked. R1-R3 was the familiar Firebolt(3)/Spark(2) loop from the old bricked wins. From R4 on, the new-deck VV density let Evo chain into Inferno Lance (4, VV), Concussive Blast (3+reaction-tax, VS), Hex Bolt (3, VV, prepped but never cast) and Fireball (5, VV, prepped R7 but never cast — doom won first). That is a materially harder late curve than the Spark/Firebolt-only pressure that padded the old m1-4/m16-18 wins — Inferno Lance alone took me from HP5 to HP1 in one hit. The new decks did make Evo's nukes land more reliably, as expected.

BRICK RATE vs old references: still present, and still decisive — NOT eliminated by the new decks, contrary to hope. Observed opponent brick-turns (passed with no cast despite having a live turn): R2-T7, R2-T9, R2-T11 (three in a row, likely a genuine component drought — Foretell's hand-reveal that round showed them empty), R4-T20, and critically R6-T37 and R7-T39 — the two turns immediately after I dropped to HP1 and HP2/HP1, where a single Searing Word/Hex Bolt/Fireball would have killed me outright and they had at least one open cast slot both times. The win was NOT clean: at HP1 for the last turn-and-a-half of the game, survival depended entirely on the bot not finding/funding a 1-damage spell on two consecutive opportunities. Per the pilot-gap doctrine, this edge is still brick-assisted even under the redesigned decks — the new decks changed *how hard* Evo hits when it connects, not reliably *whether* it connects every turn it could.

OMEN CADENCE: cast in R1, R3, R4 (3 of 6 rounds) — missed R2 entirely because early single-card draws got prioritized into Foretell/Anticipate/Premonition instead (a real resource-scarcity tradeoff, not a doctrine failure — with only 1 card/turn early, funding the MM-cost Omen competed directly with the M-cost spells). From R4 onward the loadout swapped increasingly toward Foreclosure (4-dmg doom) as the closeout tool, so "every round" cadence for Omen specifically was 3/6, not 6/6 — worth flagging that the doctrine's "ticking EVERY round" target is aspirational under real card variance, not automatic even with easier MM funding.

FINAL NOTE — SKILL VS BRICK READ: the doom-carry mechanic (Foreclosure queued R6, fired at the very start of P1's R7 turn before they could act) is what actually won the game — that part is pure structural skill/design, reproducible regardless of bot behavior. But the HP1 margin means the *timing* of the win (surviving long enough for that doom to land) rode on two bricked opponent turns at the worst possible moment for them not to brick. A cleaner test of "does Div beat new-deck Evo" would need either a higher-fidelity bot pilot for P1 or several more seeds to separate structural strength from this game's specific brick luck.
