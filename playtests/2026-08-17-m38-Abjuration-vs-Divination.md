## Analysis

HYPOTHESIS: Does skilled Abj denial+ledger play beat greedy-Div pre-fix? VERDICT: No — loss, but NOT to Div's offense. P0 died to its own deck-exhaustion penalty at HP4 (round 17), having successfully denied essentially all of Div's real damage all game (7 Foreclosure casts cancelled/sealed, 2 Entropy 7-dmg dooms sealed every round they appeared, multiple Far Sight/Omen dooms cancelled via Phase Shift/Counterbind before the prophecy even inscribed). Opponent HP finished at 24/30 — Abj was winning the attrition race on offense/denial and simply ran out of a resource nobody was tracking: the deck.

KEY DECISIVE LINES:
- Runic Seal, recast every round it was funded, reliably found and sealed the round's single scariest prepared spell (mostly Entropy DIV-032, 7 dmg pierce, or Foreclosure DIV-020, 4 dmg pierce) by exploiting the prep-phase "replace X with Y" log lines, which reveal card identity even while face-down on the board.
- Phase Shift (SS, cancels ANY spell) and Counterbind (SM, cancels M-cost spells — nearly all Div spells qualify) together canceled every Foreclosure/Far Sight/Omen that slipped past Runic Seal's one-per-round cap, at reaction speed, stopping the prophecy from ever being inscribed (better than "prevent the damage" — it prevents the clock from starting at all).
- Sanctum (redirect targeting to Wards + buff) and stacked Ward bodies (Bulwark Shard/Aegis Charm gambits, cost-free of the 1-spell/turn cap) kept a large HP buffer (peaked at 11+5+3=19 Ward HP) that never got to matter because the killing blow was non-targeted, pierce-immune deck exhaustion.
- Round-end slot exhaustion (attaching mana until "slots exhausted — opponent gets one final turn") repeatedly stranded 1-3 of Div's loaded-but-uncast prep slots for free across rounds 1, 2, 4, 5 — a real, reproducible bot blind spot exploited by forcing my own slot cap early.

LEDGER ECONOMY DATA (secondary task):
- Bank-per-round was near-zero almost every round. Stone Stance (-2 reduction) absorbed Foretell (2 dmg) fully several times (~rounds 1-9), each instance banking ~2 prevented — call it roughly 10-14 total banked prevention across the whole 17-round game from Stone Stance alone; Echo Shield/Grounding contributed a few more points early.
- Warding Tithe was cast 4 times (rounds 3, 4, 5, 8 — wards funded at 4, 2, 4[implied], 2 HP respectively) consuming essentially the ENTIRE bank as it was generated — this is the core finding: Tithe (defense) and Reckoning (offense) draw from the SAME pool, and Tithe fires opportunistically every time it's funded while Reckoning only unlocks at L3/round 10+. By the time Reckoning was castable (round 10), Tithe had already spent the bank twice more, leaving Reckoning to deal 0 damage (opponent HP unchanged 28->28) — confirmed by direct observation.
- UNCHARGED DOOM DAMAGE (would-be ledger credit under a half-rate pierce-feeds-ledger fix): tally of all doom/pierce hits taken this game: Saboteur's Kit gambit doom hit 3 times (2 dmg each = 6), Foreclosure landed unopposed once for 4 (round 14, no funded reaction that turn), Entropy landed unopposed once for 7 (round 10, Phase Shift was underfunded when it was cast), Far Sight landed its immediate-2 once (round 11, before Sanctum/Stone Stance was up) — total uncharged pierce/doom damage actually taken ≈ 6+4+7+2 = 19 points. At a proposed half-rate, that's +9-10 to the lifetime bank, which would have funded 1-2 additional Warding Tithe casts or made Reckoning non-trivial (≈5 dmg) instead of 0. This is a real, measurable gap the fix would close, but it would NOT have changed this game's outcome — the loss was to deck exhaustion, not to being out-damaged.
- Sealed Verdict was prepared/funded multiple times as insurance but never actually needed to fire (Phase Shift/Counterbind always arrived first) — dead weight in this game, though its funding cost (S) was cheap enough not to matter.

MANA DRAIN: never prepared this game (opportunity cost of an S slot against a hand-disruption tool with lower priority than direct-damage denial); no data point to report.

BOT BLIND SPOT / RULE OBSERVATIONS:
1. CRITICAL — self-inflicted deck exhaustion is a real, scaling, unavoidable death clock (2, 4, 6 damage on successive reshuffles) that is NOT gated behind opponent skill at all. A defensively-dominant Abjuration deck that spends many turns attaching/detaching components and reacting can burn through its resource deck just from normal per-turn draws over a 17-round game, and the escalating penalty becomes lethal regardless of board state. This deserves explicit design attention — either as an intended long-game clock (in which case Abj's denial plan needs an escape valve, e.g., Sealed Vault or deck-thinning discipline) or as an oversight the pilot should have anticipated earlier and actively managed (I did try to avoid unnecessary draws in the back half but the passive per-turn draw is unavoidable and cumulative).
2. Attach-cap anomaly: multi-pip identical-cost spells (Absolute Defense, SSSS) appeared capped at exactly 2 attachment actions regardless of remaining unmet cost, even though the same slot happily accepted attachments beyond its raw pip requirement when using dual/triple-pip cards (Sanctum SSS accepted [SS, SS] = 4 pips into a 3-pip cost). This blocked Absolute Defense from ever being castable via single-pip (CMP-S) components. Flagging as a suspected engine bug worth checking in packages/engine's attach-limit logic — search for whatever caps attachment COUNT rather than remaining cost.
3. Prep-phase transparency: swap/replace log lines ("P1: replace X with Y") leak card identity for cards that remain face-down on the board, which a sighted pilot can exploit for near-perfect Runic Seal targeting. This is either intended (public replacement log) or a redaction gap — worth checking against the intended information model.
4. Prophecy-cancellation timing: cancelling a doom-spell (Foreclosure, Entropy, Far Sight, Omen) via Phase Shift/Counterbind BEFORE it resolves stops the prophecy from ever being inscribed — meaning reactive counters are strictly better than reactive damage-prevention against the whole doom family, since they deny 100% of the future damage, not just the current tick. This was the single highest-leverage tactic in the game and should inform any future balance pass: Div's doom plan is entirely neutralizable by 2-3 well-timed Reaction spells if Abj can keep them funded every turn (which is the real bottleneck — funding discipline, not target availability).

RESULT SUMMARY: LOSS, round 17, P0 HP -2 vs P1 HP 24 — but the loss was to a self-inflicted mechanic (deck exhaustion), not to being out-fought. Denial discipline (seal the biggest known threat, cancel everything else that slips through, eat only the small dooms) held Div's offense to a near standstill for 16 rounds. This suggests the 0-3 starvation verdict from m31-33 is NOT fully robust: with disciplined seal/cancel play a game CAN reach a state where Abjuration is winning the damage race outright — the open question the run surfaces is deck/resource-longevity management, which is a different axis than the Reckoning-starvation problem the pending pierce-to-ledger fix targets. A rematch with more conservative card-drawing (avoid Arcane Study, minimize any avoidable draw) might convert this exact defensive line into a win.
