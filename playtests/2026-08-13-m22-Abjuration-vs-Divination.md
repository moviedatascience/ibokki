## Analysis

### RESULT: LOSS for Abjuration, round 12. Final HP: Abj -2 / Div 16.

Kill shot: Entropy [DIV-032] (L3, MMM, 7 damage in 3 turns), which resolved with zero denial available because my entire armed reaction suite (Runic Seal loaded, Phase Shift funded, Counterbind funded, Absorb funded, Mana Drain funded, Ward Collapse half-funded — six attached component cards) was swept at the R11→R12 round transition. This is a direct pilot error, not a matchup finding: CLAUDE.md's doctrine explicitly calls out "detach-rescue your reaction fuel before sweeps," and m5 (the win reference) demonstrated the technique three times. I never did it once this game, and the one round I skipped it is the round that killed me.

### Does the skilled-Abj verdict survive the faster doom clock? Inconclusive — need games 2-3.

The line itself (deny every affordable round, eat small dooms, bank wards, close with Ward Collapse) worked exactly as designed for 11 of 12 rounds and produced the same signature result the m5-m7 series found: **a fully-armed reaction suite froze the greedy bot solid.** Round 11 saw Div hold 0/3 cast slots the entire round, discarding to hand cap five times rather than commit a spell into denial range — the deterrence finding reproduced live, unprompted, under the new decks. The loss came from a bookkeeping lapse at the one moment the technique needed to be exercised (round-end, six pieces of fuel on the table), not from the denial toolkit being outpaced. Whether "arguably Abj-favored" survives the new decks is still an open question that this game's cause of death doesn't actually answer — a rerun with correct fuel-rescue discipline is the real test, and it's what games 2-3 should establish.

### The doom clock IS measurably faster than the m5-m7 references

- **Omen** (MM, 2 dmg) was cast **4 times** across the match (R1, R3, R8, R9) — every round Div could afford it and I didn't pre-seal it. In m5 (old decks) it landed only 3 times across 10 rounds, and the bot spent long stretches completely passive because it couldn't re-fund Omen. Here, with 8 MM duals in the resource deck, Div refunded MM same-round almost every time.
- **Foreclosure** (MM, 4 dmg — an L2 card, not seen at all in the m5-m7 references at this pace) was cast **3 times** (R5, R6, R7) — once even the SAME round it got sealed once (R6: sealed Omen, still ate a fresh Foreclosure + a stacked-in old one for 6 damage in one round).
- **Entropy** (MMM, 7 dmg) arrived at L3 (round 10, as predicted) and, unlike Foreclosure/Omen, was never seen or seal-able in advance — it appeared from Div's hand fully funded and cast in one motion the instant my board was bare.
- **Saboteur's Kit** (gambit, 2 dmg, bypasses the stack) fired **4 times** — this vector is unaffected by deck redesign since it's a gambit, but stacks with everything above.
- Net: over 12 rounds I took prophecy/gambit damage on 9 separate landings (2+2+2+4+4+2+2+2+7 = 27 raw before ward absorption), against roughly the same or fewer denial windows than the m5 game got in 10 rounds. The MM-dual density is doing exactly what the brief predicted.

### Ward Collapse battery — worked once, but Unbind is real tech against it

Landed **one** Ward Collapse for **7 damage** (R10: 25→18 wasn't right — actual line was 7HP ward → opp 25→18). The battery reached 12-13 HP twice (R6, R7) before either paying off or being destroyed:
- R6: Counterbind saved a 12HP ward from Unbind — the battery survived to keep growing.
- R7: the SAME battery (now 13HP) was destroyed by a second Unbind cast when Counterbind/Phase Shift were both unfunded that turn. Total loss, zero conversion.
- R10: a freshly-rebuilt 7HP ward got Collapsed for 7 real damage — the only offense Abj dealt all game beyond Ward Pulse chip (6 casts × 1 dmg = 6) and 2× opponent fatigue reshuffle damage.

Unlike the m5 game (Div never drew/prepped Unbind, so the ward pile grew uncontested to 29 HP by round 9), this Div deck had Unbind prepared from round 6 onward and used it twice. **Easier MM assembly cuts both ways**: it accelerates Div's dooms AND their ward-hate. The battery line is real but fragile — it needs simultaneous Counterbind/Phase Shift coverage to survive to a big payoff, which competes directly for the same SS/SM fuel the doom-denial line needs every round.

### Denial economics observed
- **Runic Seal**: cast 6 times (Omen×3, unknown facedown×3), one confirmed misread (R5: guessed the wrong facedown slot and let a live Foreclosure through — slot identity is NOT inferable from prep-log replacement order).
- **Mana Drain**: fired 4 times, always stripping the opponent's first attach of the turn; never stopped a cast outright but reliably taxed their tempo.
- **Counterbind**: 1 cast, saved a 12HP ward from Unbind — highest single-swing value of the game.
- **Phase Shift**: funded repeatedly but never actually fired (Runic Seal or Counterbind kept beating it to the punch, or fuel ran out first) — in this game it was insurance that was never called on, unlike m5 where it was the 3-cancel MVP.
- **Absorb**: prepared R8-R12, funded multiple times, never cast — never found a spell worth spending it on, and never got to test whether it can blunt Prophecy damage (never resolved that open question from mid-game).
- Net effect: the denial suite reliably ate ONE MM threat per round when I had SS/SM in hand at the right moment; it could not cover two simultaneous MM threats in the same round (which happened routinely from R5 onward — Div consistently had 2-3 MM slots funded at once), and it could not survive a missed detach-rescue at all.

### Anomalies / engine notes
1. **Fatigue is real and symmetric**: reshuffling an empty resource deck costs 2 direct HP damage to whoever reshuffles (confirmed for both P0 at R8/9 cycle and P1 at R11/12 cycle). This is a previously-undocumented mechanic worth flagging for the rules doc if it isn't already there.
2. **Facedown prepared-spell identity cannot be inferred from prep-phase replacement log order** — costly to learn mid-game; scouting requires Mana Drain triggers or attach-pattern reads, not log bookkeeping.
3. **Component-attach state does not survive a round transition unless explicitly detached** — the R12 death confirms this is not just a UI quirk but the actual rule, and it is unforgiving: a maximally-loaded defensive board can be reset to zero by the clock alone, independent of anything the opponent does.
4. **Deterrence reproduces under the new decks**: a fully-armed reaction suite (4-5 pieces) froze the greedy bot for an entire round (R11) exactly as documented in the m5-m7 pilot findings, even though its deck now assembles dooms faster. This suggests the bot's freeze behavior is triggered by visible threat density rather than raw doom-output rate, and is not something the deck redesign changed.
5. **Saboteur's Kit chip damage is unaffected by "frozen" bot behavior** — the bot kept playing this uncancellable gambit doom during the R11 freeze even while declining every spell cast, confirming the freeze is specific to stack-vulnerable spell casting, not to all forms of pressure.
