## Analysis

**Result / margin:** P1 (Abjuration) wins on HP, round 12. Final HP: P0 -23, P1 3. Margin was razor-thin on my own life total (down to 3 HP at the death) but the opponent's HP total never recovered from the two Reckoning bursts (20 dmg in R10, 24 in R12).

**Decisive lines:**
- The Stone Stance → Arcane Shell → Fortify loop (3 prep slots, 1 cast/turn) held chip damage to near-zero for 9 rounds while banking prevented damage every time a ward soaked a hit.
- Reckoning (ABJ-032, unlocked at level 10) cashed the bank twice: 20 damage in R10 (opponent 30→10, then a self-inflicted exhaustion tick took them to 6), and a second Reckoning for 24 in R12 for the kill at 1 HP. The prevention ledger is NOT consumed/reset by casting Reckoning — I recast it in R12 off a much smaller intervening bank window and it still one-shot a 1-HP target, confirming the ledger persists/accumulates across the whole match and Reckoning reads the running total each time, not a delta.
- Banishing Bolt (ABJ-035, SSS, 5/7 dmg) closed 6 HP → 1 HP once Reckoning had done the heavy lifting.
- Late-game near-death: opponent's Dispelling Powder trainer sniped my ward right before Wrath of the Mage, denying a planned Ward Pulse kill and costing me a Stance-less full hit; I dropped to 3 HP before closing it out. This was the closest call of the series and worth flagging — the mitigation shell is not proof against artifact/trainer-based ward destruction.

**HYPOTHESIS VERDICT:** Piloted Abjuration found its edge comfortably despite the Evo buffs. The mitigation loop pre-buff and post-buff plays almost identically because Stone Stance's flat -2 keeps pace with the new damage floors (Fireball 6→4, Lance 5→3 under Stance) as long as ward HP is sized to the post-Stance number, not the raw number. Reckoning is the actual finisher and it's completely blind to the Evo damage buffs — it converts prevented damage 1:1 regardless of what raw number produced it, so the ledger scaled up in lockstep with the bigger Evo hits (bigger Fireballs banked bigger chunks). The +1 bumps if anything modestly HELPED my win condition since more raw damage into a full ward = more banked prevention = a bigger eventual Reckoning.

**Ward sizing math post-buff:** confirmed 5 HP ward fully eats a post-Stance Fireball (4) with 1 to spare; 3 HP ward exactly eats a post-Stance Lance (3) or an un-Stanced-but-partially-mitigated Wrath of the Mage. The old "3-ward eats an old 3-Firebolt" heuristic still works fine post-buff as long as you remember Fireball's post-Stance floor moved from 3 to 4 — I had one round (R8) where a 3 HP ward only just barely undersized against a full 6-dmg Fireball landing before I got to recast Stance (spillover of 3 HP), reinforcing that the KEY risk isn't ward sizing, it's ROUND PARITY — whichever side goes first in a given round gets to land one hit before the defender's Stone Stance is back up. I ate two "cold" full-damage hits (R2, R8/R9 Fireballs, R6/R9 Lances) purely because Stance hadn't been recast yet when the opponent had first-turn priority that round. This is a structural tempo cost of the -2-per-round design, not a card-power issue.

**Stoke / Mana Burn:** Stoke (EVO-006) never appeared — the bot never needed V recursion since it kept drawing fresh components. Mana Burn (EVO-029) DID appear and DID matter: the bot correctly held it as a prepared reaction and used it reactively against my Stone Stance cast in R11, even though I had attached VS (no M component) to Stone Stance. It dealt its unconditional 2 damage but did NOT cancel Stone Stance (which still resolved) — this matches the card's actual text structure ("cancel target spell OR reaction that requires an M component; deal 2 damage") where the 2 damage is unconditional and only the cancel clause is gated on the M requirement. Worth flagging to the reviewer as a possible misread risk: a human/bot skimming the card might expect the whole effect to fizzle against a non-M target; here it partially fired (damage only), which is correct per the text but easy to misplay around.

**Bot blind spots observed:**
1. The greedy bot did not recognize game-ending lethal risk when passing on R12 T82-T84 while sitting at 1 HP with a live Fireball/Lance in prep — it had two full turns where casting either would have been irrelevant to the outcome (both had already dropped me to 3 HP, but its earlier pass at T82 with the board in a position where I was already at 3 HP and defenseless was a missed kill window; the bot instead played nothing that turn).
2. The bot never valued/pursued a Detonate/hand-size payoff or Sunburst-style anti-ward answer in this seed — its own deck composition this game leaned on Fireball/Lance/Wrath repeats plus Cataclysm/Dispelling Powder, never actually punishing the ward-stacking plan directly except via the two Dispelling Powder casts (R2, R12), both of which were well-timed but not followed up with lethal pressure.
3. Reckoning is a card the bot has no visibility into pricing (it's an Abjuration card) but structurally the bot never played around "opponent HP suddenly craters" — no attempt to race faster or hold burn for lethal once its own HP dropped under my archetype's typical Reckoning payout size (~20).

**Suspected rule/economy notes (not necessarily bugs):** Reckoning's ledger reads as a persistent running total across the whole match, uncapped and not consumed by prior casts — this is a very strong design lever once unlocked (L10) and is likely THE core Abjuration win condition in this deck build rather than direct-damage spells; worth flagging to design as intentional-and-central rather than a fringe tech card.
