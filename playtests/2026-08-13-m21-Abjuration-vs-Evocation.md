## Analysis

RESULT: WIN, round 15. Final HP P0:14 P1:-47 (i.e. Evo was sitting at 13 the instant Final Reckoning landed for 60). Margin at the moment of the kill was razor-thin — +1 HP — a sharp contrast with m8's clean 29-0 blowout on the old decks.

DID THE ABJ EDGE SURVIVE THE NEW DECKS: yes, but the character of the win changed completely. The ward-stack/Stone-Stance shell (never skipped — Stone Stance cast in all 15 rounds, beating even m8's 14-of-15 record) again absorbed the overwhelming majority of Evo's output: of ~15 rounds of chip/burst, only five events ever touched my HP directly (Firebolt -3 R1 before Stance was up, Inferno Lance -2 R9, my own exhaustion -2 R11, Phoenix Ascendant -5 R15, Apocalypse -12 R15 unpreventable). Everything else — Spark, Burning Hands, Searing Word, repeated Fireball/Inferno Lance/Wrath of the Mage/Meteor/Chain Lightning — was fully or mostly eaten by Stone Stance + a constantly-rebuilt ward stack (peaked at three simultaneous wards, 6/6/3, in R13-14). The game was won on defense exactly as doctrine predicted. What changed is that Evo's late-game finishers (Phoenix Ascendant, Apocalypse) actually landed real, unpreventable-or-nearly-so damage in the same round, turning what should have been another blowout into a photo finish — the win came down to Abjuration's own finisher (Final Reckoning) resolving one round before Evo could follow up its Apocalypse with anything else.

RECKONING CADENCE — the headline finding: Reckoning [ABJ-032] (the SS repeatable half-of-lifetime-prevented finisher the brief asked me to track) was prepped at L10 (R10) and never cast, not once, across R10-R15. Every round I had SS-capable duals in hand, they went to competing SS-cost defensive spells instead — Absorb (funded R8, R14-15) and Reflective Ward (funded and cast R7, R10, R13) — because the new Evo deck's reaction suite (Combust, Final Riposte, and eventually Pyromancer's Reckoning) made "is it safe to spend my turn on offense" a live question almost every round, and those defensive SS spells felt more urgent than banking a Reckoning cast. By R15, level-up unlocked Final Reckoning (SSSM, 4 dmg/round-elapsed, uncapped) which looked like a faster and safer kill than trying to also fund and fire Reckoning, so I pivoted entirely. Net effect: the brief's expected "refuels for one card per round" cadence never got observed in this game — Reckoning sat prepped-and-idle for 6 straight rounds while a different finisher (available 5 levels later) did the actual killing. This is a real doctrine gap, not a card problem: with 8 SS duals the fuel supply for Reckoning was there, but it had to queue behind genuinely necessary defensive SS spells whose absence would have cost real HP, and the doctrine (m8-m10 vintage) never accounted for that competition existing on the new, faster-clocked Evo deck.

NEW-DECK CLOCK SPEED vs the old 90%+ read: substantially faster and scarier. In m8 (old deck, seed 3101) Evo's entire game output was 3 face damage before dying to exhaustion at R10 — it never assembled a real finisher. Here, Evo reached Fireball/Wrath of the Mage/Inferno Lance by R5-9 (trivial VV/VM funding off the new duals), then L3-4 finishers — Meteor (7), Chain Lightning (4+ward-shred), Cataclysm, Combustive Sigil, and finally Apocalypse (12, flatly unpreventable) and Phoenix Ascendant (6+2 dmg buff, doubles burn) — by R12-15. Direct face damage taken across the match: 24 HP worth of real hits (not counting ward-absorbed damage), versus essentially 0 sustained damage in the old-deck game beyond the single opening Firebolt. The new cross-dual split (6 VM for Evo per the brief) clearly accelerated their mid-tier assembly a lot; Apocalypse specifically is a genuine answer to the ward-stacking Abj shell (bypasses both Stone Stance and Wards outright) and very nearly closed the gap to a loss.

NEW-DECK SHAPE EFFECTS (Abjuration side): the 2 VS + 6 SM cross-dual split meant literally every component I ever drew carried at least one S pip (CMP-S, CMP-SM, CMP-SS, CMP-VS, CMP-VSM all qualify) — Stone Stance/Fortify/Ward Pulse (all pure-S cost) were NEVER fuel-starved in 15 rounds, a strictly better assembly rate than the old deck. The bottleneck moved entirely to the SS/SSSM tier: Absorb, Reflective Ward, Reckoning, and especially Final Reckoning regularly needed 2 turns to complete because the "max 2 component cards per spell" rule meant I needed either one CMP-SS outright or two separate S-bearing cards, and a specific M-pip card (for Final Reckoning) wasn't always in hand when the S half was ready (R15: had SS funded turn 1, had to wait an extra turn for a CMP-SM to arrive before completing SSSM). This tiered friction (trivial L1 assembly, real friction at SS+) looks like intentional and healthy design.

PRICING NOTES: (1) Final Reckoning (SSSM) is an extreme late-game outlier — uncapped, unconditional 4 dmg per round elapsed with no scaling cost, so by R15 it is a flat 60 for a single spell; whichever side reaches it and gets a clean window simply wins. Worth a balance look independent of school. (2) Apocalypse (VVVV, 12 unpreventable/uncancellable/unreducible) is good, necessary counterplay to ward-stacking Abj — it's the only Evo tool all game that fully bypassed the shell — but on a passive greedy-bot pilot it arrived too late (R15) to be decisive; a more aggressive human/pilot Evo opponent assembling it by R10-12 would likely flip this specific game. (3) Ward Pulse has no fallback when you control zero Wards (unlike Fortify's "if none, create one" clause) — I cast it into an empty board once (R15, right after Apocalypse) and it fully whiffed, wasting a cast slot at the worst possible moment. Worth a design look, or at minimum a pilot-side "check ward count first" rule for the doctrine.

REACTION-TRIGGER ENFORCEMENT (today's engine change): held up cleanly — no observed instance of Combust firing off a spell cast, no observed instance of my spell-triggered reactions firing off anything but a spell. No illegal reaction triggers seen in either direction.

ENGINE / DAMAGE-MATH ANOMALIES (flagged, not fully diagnosed — worth an engine-side look): (1) Mana Burn (EVO-029, cancels a spell "that requires a Material component") reacted onto my Reflective Ward (SS-funded, zero M anywhere) in R7 — the cancel correctly fizzled (Reflective Ward still resolved), but its unconditional "deal 2 damage" clause only registered 1 point of ward loss (2 HP ward → 1 HP) instead of 2; mechanism unclear (possible partial Stone Stance interaction on Reaction-sourced damage, since Stone Stance's text says "spell damage" and Mana Burn is a Reaction). (2) One Fireball cast under an active Stone Stance (R8/R9 boundary) cleanly destroyed a 4-HP ward with zero spillover, implying only 1 net point of reduction landed (expected 5-2=3, which should have left the ward at 1 HP, not 0) — inconsistent with every other Fireball/Wrath-of-the-Mage/Meteor instance in the same game, which all showed a clean, correct -2. Likely a one-off rather than systemic (Stone Stance behaved correctly in roughly a dozen other instances), but it's a repeatable-looking discrepancy worth a targeted regression test. (3) Chain Lightning (R14: "deal 4 to face, then deal 2 to EACH Ward") produced wards[6,6,3]→[4,1] with my HP completely unchanged — better for me than a literal reading of the card text suggests (only 2 of 3 wards ended up affected, one fully destroyed with a Reflective Ward proc landing 1 dmg back on Evo), but the exact routing of the "4 to face" clause through Stone Stance and/or ward-absorption-by-default wasn't legible from the compact log and deserves a verbose-mode trace.

BOT BLINDSPOTS: (1) the greedy Evo bot repeatedly fully-funded reactions (Combust-cost VV, Final-Riposte-cost VVM patterns) then let multiple turns pass without ever using or unfunding them, eventually losing that fuel to the round-end sweep — the same "hoard armed fuel into the sweep" blindspot documented in m8, still present on the new decks. (2) it let me stack three simultaneous Wards (R13-14) without ever using a second Dispelling Powder or any other ward-destroy tool to deny the stack proactively — it played Dispelling Powder exactly twice all game (R3, R15), both reactively rather than as denial. (3) it spent R13-14 passing and building its hand to 9-10 cards rather than pressuring me while my ward buffer was thin, handing me the exact multi-turn window needed to assemble Final Reckoning's SSSM cost.

VERDICT ON THE EDGE: Abjuration still wins this pairing on the new decks, and the core doctrine (Stone Stance every round, ward-stack absorbs everything, attach-first blanks the discard-on-hit cantrips) is intact and, if anything, easier to execute now that every Abj component carries an S pip. But the margin genuinely narrowed: this was a 1-HP-margin race decided by which finisher landed first, not a blowout, and it happened only because Evo is bot-piloted and passive — a sharper Evo pilot racing its own new, much faster VVVV/VVVM tier could plausibly flip it. Combined with the bot-level move from 53%→50/50, this piloted result reads as "Abj > Evo still holds at skill, but the gap the pilots estimated at 90%+ on the old decks has compressed hard on the new ones" — worth weighting alongside game 1 and 2 of this series rather than treating as the whole story.
