## Analysis

## Abjuration (piloted) vs Divination (greedy bot) — seed 6203 — NEW-DECK calibration

**RESULT: LOSS for Abjuration, round 10. Final HP: Abj 0 / Div 30.** Same pilot, same doctrine, same tool-kit as the old-deck 3-0 sweep (m5-m7) — and this time it wasn't close. HP path: 30→28(R1)→25(R3)→21(R4)→17(R5)→15(R6)→9(R7)→7(R7)→7 through all of R8→8(R9, post-heal)→6(R9)→4(R9)→2(R10)→0(R10). Gross damage taken over the game was roughly **34–35 HP** against a starting pool of 30 (+5 from one Second Wind), a ~4x increase over m5's 9 total damage taken across the same 10 rounds with a *lighter* denial commitment.

### Does the skilled-Abj verdict survive? No.
The old-deck verdict ("arguably Abj-favored" at skill) does **not** survive the new-deck doom clock. This was not a piloting error or a missed line — the denial kit was played close to optimally (see scorecard below: 13 successful denials in 10 rounds, essentially one per round-and-a-half) and Abjuration still died. The new resource decks (8x MM duals for Divination) make Omen/Foreclosure assembly so cheap and so parallel that Divination can threaten **two simultaneous doom slots per round** (confirmed R4: Omen + Saboteur's Kit both queued same turn; R7: Foreclosure(4) stacked under an already-queued 2, then Far Sight added a 3rd) while Abjuration's three answer tools (Runic Seal, Phase Shift, Counterbind) can each only stop one cast per round at best. The arithmetic doesn't close: Divination can *generate* threats faster than Abjuration can *fund* answers.

### What actually killed the game
1. **Foreclosure went from absent to omnipresent.** In m5 (old deck), Divination's AI never even prepped Foreclosure across 10 rounds — Omen was its only doom. In m24 (new deck), Div swapped Foreclosure in at R5 and it stayed a live, re-armable 4-damage threat every round after. Foreclosure alone connected 3 times (R5, R7, R9) for 12 direct damage — the single biggest damage source in the game, and something the old-deck reference line never had to deal with at all. This is exactly the brief's hypothesis: easier MM assembly changed not just *speed* but *deck composition/AI preference*.
2. **The first-action doom window recurred, unpredictably.** Turn order alternates by round in a way that isn't controllable from the Abjuration side. It hit twice: R1 (Omen, unanswerable at L1 prep — expected) and **R7 (Foreclosure, 4 dmg, fully unanswerable despite having Runic Seal/Phase Shift/Counterbind all prepped)** — the round-10-cap kit doesn't help if Divination simply acts before Abjuration gets a single turn.
3. **A new failure mode: resource drought.** By R9 sustained denial spending (Runic Seal every round, Phase Shift and Counterbind pre-funded defensively) had emptied Abjuration's 40-card resource deck. At R9T67-68 the hand held zero component cards — Foreclosure landed completely unanswered not because the tool wasn't prepped, but because there was no fuel to pay for it. This never happened in the old-deck sweep; the new-deck matchup taxes the resource base harder because it forces answering (or eating) far more casts per game.
4. **Reactions must be pre-funded — confirmed and costly.** Reactions (Phase Shift, Counterbind, Echo Shield) require SS/SM/S already attached *before* the triggering cast; there is no "fund on the fly" option at the stack window (confirmed R5T35: Foreclosure hit the stack with Phase Shift prepped-but-unfunded, and the only legal action was `pass`). This forces spending main-phase actions on speculative pre-funding every single turn, which is itself part of what drained the resource deck (finding #3).
5. **Doom/prophecy damage is completely unpreventable once cast — confirmed by direct test.** Echo Shield ("prevent 1/2 damage") was tried both at the delayed-payout moment (R9T72, no reaction window offered at all — it auto-resolved) and during the triggering cast's own resolution (R9T76, explicitly reacted with Echo Shield while Omen was on the stack — the prophecy still inscribed for full damage). Wards likewise never reduced doom damage. **The only lever against a doom, ever, is denying the cast itself** (Runic Seal pre-emptively, or Phase Shift/Counterbind reactively at cast time) — prevention/absorption tools are real but only work against ordinary direct-damage spells (Foretell), not prophecies.
6. **Ward Collapse arrived too late to matter.** The 18-HP ward battery (9 rounds of Fortify/Arcane Shell/Bulwark Shard/Aegis Charm) hit its L3 unlock exactly on schedule (level 10, matching m5's finding) — but by round 10 HP was already at 2-4 from the accelerated doom clock. Ward Collapse got prepped but never cast; there was no round left to cash it. The battery plan's fixed unlock timing (round 10) didn't move, but the survival budget to reach it shrank by roughly 4x.

### Denial economics (13 successful denials, still lost)
- **Runic Seal: 8 casts, all successful** (sealed a doom-shaped or fully-fueled slot every single time it was cast: R1, R2, R3, R4, R5, R6-Foreclosure, R7-Omen, R8-Foreclosure). This is double the cast count of the old-deck game (4) — direct evidence of needing to seal far more often just to keep pace.
- **Phase Shift: funded 4 times, used only once** (R7T54, cancelled a lethal-adjacent Foretell). Contrast m5 where Phase Shift was the load-bearing card with 3 cancels — here it was mostly held in reserve (per doctrine, saving it for bigger threats) and by the time bigger threats needed it, resources had run out to keep it loaded.
- **Counterbind: 4 successful cancels** (Unbind-R6, Far Sight-R7, Far Sight-R8, Foretell-R10 — the last one saved the game from ending 2 turns early). New-deck's 6 SM duals made it easy to keep this loaded, and it did the most reactive work of the three tools.
- **Echo Shield: 2 reactions, 1 useful** (fully absorbed a 2-dmg Foretell R8; confirmed useless against prophecy damage R9).
- Despite 13/13 successful denials landing exactly as intended, Divination still dealt enough unanswered damage (Foreclosure x3, Omen x6, Saboteur's Kit x2 gambit-uncancellable, Far Sight x2, Foretell x1 partial) to win by round 10.

### Anomalies / mechanic notes
- **Sealed Vault (Div gambit) resets deck-out risk.** Divination's deck bottomed at 6 cards (R8) then Sealed Vault reshuffled it back to 36 — removing what looked like a developing decking-out race in Abjuration's favor. Abjuration has no equivalent effect in this kit; Abjuration's own deck hit 0 first (R9) and had to auto-reshuffle (an "exhaustion" counter, now at 2 — mechanic exists but didn't cost HP directly beyond the coincident timing).
- **Mana Sickness (Div gambit) as a denial counter-tool**, played at Abjuration's 2-HP nadir (R10) to block bonus/gambit-driven card draws — sharp, correctly-timed disruption by the bot right when it mattered most.
- **Disarm (Div gambit)** relocates a targeted component from Abjuration's hand to the top of Abjuration's own deck (not lost, just resequenced) — appeared twice, minor tempo tool, not a major factor this game.
- Overfunding (2-card max, using a triple/dual component on a 1-pip spell) appeared from the bot several times (VSM on M-cost Foretell) — cosmetic bot inefficiency, not exploitable this game.

### Verdict
**The skilled-Abj-favored verdict from m5-m7 does NOT survive the new resource decks.** The denial line itself is unchanged and was executed at a high standard (8/8 Runic Seals landed, 4/4 Counterbinds landed, correct triage of which slot to seal every round) — the loss is a resource-arithmetic problem, not a piloting or doctrine problem: Divination's 8 MM duals let it field Foreclosure as a *permanent* second doom threat (never seen in the old deck) and occasionally double-doom a single turn, while Abjuration's three-tool denial kit can only stop one cast per round and drains its own resource deck doing so. Bot-level 77% Div is not overstating it here — if anything a skilled pilot only delayed the loss (13 clean denials, held at HP 15+ through round 6) rather than reversing it. Recommend: either slow Divination's MM-dual density back down, or give Abjuration a second denial action per round / a way to answer the first-action doom window, before calling this matchup balanced.
