## Analysis

### Result

P0 (Abjuration) wins on HP, round 11. Final HP: P0 30, P1 -12 (Reckoning overkill). Margin:
total blowout — I never dropped below 24/30 HP all game, and the last Reckoning alone dealt
more than twice the opponent's remaining HP pool. This is a much wider margin than the m21
photo finish (1 HP); the ledger family, played defensively, produced a dominant rather than
close win.

### Ledger spends (the three new cards)

1. R3 T11 — Warding Tithe, spent 4 → new 4-HP ward. Bank income at that point was already
   ample (Stone Stance + one ward-absorb + Grounding had banked ~9).
2. R4 T24 — Warding Tithe, spent 4 → new 4-HP ward (third ward layer).
3. R7 T50 — Warding Tithe, spent 4 → new 4-HP ward (bank comfortably >15 at that point).
4. R8 T62 — Warding Tithe, spent 4 → new 4-HP ward.
5. R9 T76 — Restoring Rune, spent 6 → healed 3 (27→30 HP, topped off).
6. R9 T70 — explicit HOLD: declined a Tithe cast to preserve bank ahead of the L10 Reckoning
   unlock (the one deliberate spend-vs-hold decision this game — see verdict below on why it
   barely mattered).

Total ledger spend: 4×4 (Tithe) + 6 (Rune) = **22 points spent** across the game.
Sealed Verdict was sealed twice (R5, R8) but never actually cast — 0 points spent there.

### The two Reckoning casts (the payoff)

- R10 T79: Reckoning dealt **18 damage** (bank was ~35-36 pre-cast, ceil/2=18). Dropped
  opponent 26→8.
- R11 T85: Reckoning dealt **20 damage** (bank ~39-40 pre-cast) — game-ending overkill
  (P1 to -12).

Gross prevented income across the match, reconstructed: roughly 57-60+ points over 10 rounds
(22 spent + ~35-38 still banked for the second Reckoning; damage scaled 18→20 between the two
casts, consistent with continued income between R10 and R11 rather than a full bank reset on
cast).

### Verdict on whether optionality worked

**No — not in this game.** The central hypothesis (does spending on Tithe/Verdict/Rune create
a real scarcity trade-off against Reckoning) did not hold up under play. Income from Stone
Stance (-2/round, every round), Echo Shield (-2 per react with a ward up), Grounding
(-1 per react + draw), and ward-absorb (every point a ward eats counts) was so abundant that
I spent 22 points on defense/healing across 8 rounds and STILL had a bank north of 35 by round
10, then north of 39 by round 11. Both Reckoning casts were still lethal-scale (18, then 20 —
the second alone more than double the opponent's remaining HP). The one deliberate "hold"
decision (R9 T70, skipping a Tithe cast) turned out not to matter — the bank was so oversized
that a single skipped 4-point spend was noise against a 35+ point balance.

In other words: the ledger family doesn't compete with Reckoning for the same resource in any
binding way at these income rates. You can max out defensive/healing spending AND still
one-round a Reckoning kill once L10 hits. The "real choice between spending and holding" the
brief asked about never materialized because the bank never got close to being a binding
constraint — I was never in a position where casting one more Tithe would have meaningfully
weakened a future Reckoning's kill threshold. Cutting Tithe usage in half across the game
(I cast it 4 times) probably would have made zero difference to the final result — Reckoning
was still overkill by 12 HP.

### Did Sealed Verdict's 1-card cancel change the reaction economy?

Barely — I never actually cast it. I sealed it twice (R5, R8-R9) as a standing threat but never
pulled the trigger: every incoming spell was either (a) small enough that my ward stack ate it
for free, or (b) Hex Bolt, which is explicitly immune to being targeted by Reactions
(confirming Verdict/Phase Shift-style cancels are hard-countered by that clause, while
non-targeting preventions like Echo Shield still work on it — Echo Shield legally reacted to
Hex Bolt twice, R7 T49 and R9 T75/76-adjacent, since it prevents damage rather than targeting
the spell). The 1-card-attach restriction (vs Phase Shift's 2) never bit because I never needed
the second attach-a-component upside anyway. Verdict's real cost wasn't its 6-bank price — it
was opportunity cost of a prep slot, and even that didn't matter since I had a free slot from
R5 on. Net: Verdict added a "sleeping" defensive option that padded my sense of safety but
never entered the actual damage ledger. This game does not exercise its interaction with
Combust risk in a meaningful way either, since I always found a cheaper/safer option (ward
soak, Echo Shield, or just tanking) instead of ever pulling Verdict's trigger.

### Is the soak-refill loop degenerate or fair?

Confirmed present and generous, but not degenerate in this game specifically because I never
needed to lean on it near a knife's edge — my HP never dropped below 24/30. Every ward-soak
point refills the "prevented" bank at 1:1, and combined with Stone Stance's flat -2/round and
Echo Shield's ward-gated -2/react, the income rate comfortably outpaces a reasonable defensive
spend rate. This reproduces and *amplifies* the m19-m21-era finding (long attrition, repeated
smaller Reckonings) into something more extreme: with three new outlets to dump bank into
(which nominally should have made Reckoning smaller), the bank was instead large enough to fund
all three outlets AND deliver a bigger single Reckoning than the old doctrine's
repeated-smaller-Reckoning pattern. The ledger family did not throttle Reckoning — if anything
the richer toolkit meant I took even less damage (more walls, a heal), which meant I spent more
of my turns casting Stone Stance/Fortify/Tithe and fewer resources on offense, yet the passive
income alone was enough to still produce a lethal double-Reckoning finish by round 11.

### Anomalies

- **Fireball ward math (R6 T36):** a single 5-damage Fireball (no Stone Stance active that
  round) reduced ward totals by what looked like 6 (wards[3,4,4,4]→[1,4,4], destroying the
  3-ward and denting a 4-ward down to 1) rather than 5. Possibly overkill/rounding in how
  multi-ward soak splits, or this is a misread of which ward(s) took the hit. Worth a
  unit-test check on multi-ward damage distribution — if it's really absorbing more than the
  spell dealt, that's a small ward-side buff nobody asked for.
- **Dispelling Powder (opponent's GAM-012) destroyed a ward outright twice** (R8 T60, R9 T77)
  with no damage number attached — pure ward removal, not damage-based. This doesn't feed the
  Abjuration "prevented" ledger at all (no HP threatened, no damage event), which seems
  correct/intended, but it's a real hard-counter to the wall-building strategy this ledger
  family encourages: it deletes ward HP for free, no ledger compensation, and Evocation has
  repeatable access to it.
- **Component-deck exhaustion (R9 T64-66):** my resource deck hit 0 and reshuffled, costing 2
  flat HP (mirrored for the opponent one turn later, -2 HP). Not ledger-related, but worth
  flagging since heavy component-attach turns (which this ledger family encourages — attaching
  to 6-7 prepared slots a round) accelerate deck burn and can trigger this penalty as a side
  effect of playing the deep, multi-outlet Abjuration kit "correctly."
- **Never triggered Combust risk directly** — opponent prepared Combust openly once (R6) but
  I never called their bluff or confirmed whether the unrevealed slots stayed Combust for the
  rest of the game; I erred conservative for ~4 potential reaction windows (R8 T57, R9 T75, R9
  T77) that in hindsight were probably safe to take since the wards/Stone Stance made them
  low-value anyway. This is a piloting note more than a balance one.

### Bottom line for design

The reference doctrine still wins, decisively (round 11, opponent to -12). But the specific
question the brief posed — does the ledger family make skilled Abj play richer with real
spend-vs-hold choices — reads as **no** at this income rate. The bank grows fast enough from
routine defensive play (Stone Stance every round, Echo Shield/Grounding on demand, ward-soak
as a side effect of just blocking hits) that Tithe/Verdict/Rune are effectively "free" spends
layered on top of an untouched, still-lethal Reckoning reserve. If the design goal is a genuine
tension, the prevented-damage income rate (or Reckoning's conversion rate) likely needs to come
down, or the ledger-family spells need to cost more relative to the income rate, before holding
the bank vs. spending it becomes a decision that actually changes outcomes.
