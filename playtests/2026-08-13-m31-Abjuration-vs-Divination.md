## Analysis

### RESULT: LOSS for Abjuration, round 9. Final HP: Abj -1 / Div 30.

Kill shot: Far Sight [DIV-023] (L2, MM, 2-damage prophecy), cast completely unopposed on the final round because I had zero component fuel in hand to fund Phase Shift (its only answer that round — Sealed Verdict had already been cut by then). At HP1 heading into that turn, any unanswered prophecy was lethal; this one landed for exactly the 2 damage needed. Not a ledger-family failure specifically — a fuel-drought death at critically low HP, the kind of finish the pilot-gap doctrine predicts for the raw bot matchup (0-3 baseline) even with a stronger kit.

### Bank charge rate vs Divination: slow but real, and now precisely measured

The central question was whether skilled play can charge the "prevented" bank meaningfully against a school whose signature damage (prophecy/doom) explicitly pierces wards and reactions. Answer: **yes, but slowly, and it caps out well short of Sealed Verdict's threshold on a realistic timescale.**

- Every prophecy landing (Omen, Foreclosure, Saboteur's Kit, Far Sight — 9 separate hits over the game) contributed **zero** to the bank, confirmed directly: reacting with Grounding on Omen at R1T5 drew a card but did not reduce the inscribed prophecy from 2 to 1 — the "prevent 1" had no legal target against doom damage.
- The only bank sources that worked were exactly the ones the brief predicted: **Stone Stance's flat -2** fully absorbing two separate Foretell casts (R3, R5) with zero ward HP spent, and **ward absorption** of a third Foretell (R4, ward 6→4).
- Direct confirmation of the bank's size: casting Warding Tithe at R8T67 (after 7 full rounds of accumulation) produced a **full 4 HP ward** — the spend cap — meaning the bank had reached at least 4 by that point. This is the first hard number obtained all game; before this cast, Sealed Verdict's legality check was the only other signal, and it never fired.
- Sealed Verdict was armed (S attached) for parts of 3 different rounds (R5, R6, R7) — including R7T57, the single highest-threat-density moment of the match (Foretell/Omen/Foreclosure/Unbind all four fully funded simultaneously) — and was **never once offered as a legal reaction**. Bank stayed under 6 for the entire time it was prepped, across 3 rounds and roughly 15+ opponent turns.
- Net rate estimate: roughly **4 prevented banked in 7 rounds** (~0.6/round) from a deck built specifically to feed the bank (Stone Stance + Fortify/wards), against a Divination list that only offered 3 Foretell-class direct-damage windows in that span. Sealed Verdict needs 6, i.e. roughly **10+ rounds** of this rate to go live once — and every point spent on Tithe (or Reckoning, or Restoring Rune) resets progress toward it. The two spenders are in direct competition for the same trickle, and Verdict loses that race by design once anything else taps the bank.

### Verdict verdict: dead weight in this specific matchup, on this timescale

Sealed Verdict never went live in 9 rounds despite deliberate prep-slot investment (R5-R7) and correct piloting (armed every round it was prepped, detach-rescued every round it wasn't triggered, never wasted on a target it couldn't answer). It cost nothing but a re-attached S component per round — never blocked anything else, since S is abundant — but it also delivered nothing. Against this pierce-heavy school, the family's marquee card is a bystander unless the game runs long enough (10+ rounds) for the bank to clear 6 on its own, and Foreclosure/Entropy-class doom pressure is unlikely to let a game run that long uncontested. **Verdict is not live-fire tested as "the extra answer per round" this game; it is a trap slot that looks like insurance but never pays out against Divination specifically.** This doesn't indict the card broadly — against Evocation or a school with more preventable direct damage, the calculus would likely differ — but for this specific pilot-gap leg, it does not close the gap the brief asked about.

### Warding Tithe: the one ledger card that actually delivered

Cast once (R8T67), spending the full available bank (4) for a 4 HP ward — better HP-per-cast-slot than Fortify's flat 2 HP, using fuel (banked prevented damage) that would otherwise sit idle. This is a real, positive, non-tiny result: doctrine's warning against "tiny 1-HP Tithes" held (I never cast it until the bank had visibly built up over 7 rounds), and the payoff justified the wait. The catch: casting it **zeroed out** the bank's progress toward Sealed Verdict, and I made that trade deliberately once it became clear Verdict was not close to live and HP pressure (7 at the time) made an immediate ward more valuable than a hypothetical future cancel. In hindsight this was the correct call — Verdict was cut from prep the very next round anyway.

### Restoring Rune: never cast — bank was already spent

Swapped in at R9 (HP1, survival mode) specifically to test the heal, but the bank had been zeroed by the Tithe cast one round earlier and never recovered before the game ended one round later. No data on its in-game value beyond confirming it competes for the exact same drained resource — spending on any one ledger card directly deletes runway toward the other two.

### Denial economics (non-ledger toolkit, for context)
- **Runic Seal**: cast 6 times, correctly prioritizing known MM-funded threats (Omen, Foreclosure, Unbind) over guesses on ambiguous facedowns. Landed clean seals most rounds but could only ever stop one threat per round while Div frequently funded 2-4 simultaneously from R5 onward.
- **Mana Drain**: fired 4 times, consistently stripping the opponent's first attach each round — tempo tax, never a hard stop.
- **Stone Stance**: the unsung MVP of this game — fully no-sold 2 of 3 Foretell casts and fed the only reliable bank income. Recast every round it could be funded.
- **Phase Shift**: 1 cast (R8T72), cancelling Far Sight before its prophecy could be inscribed — the single highest-value defensive play of the game, executed correctly on a doom-causing spell per doctrine (cancel the CAST, not the arrival, to deny the inscription entirely).
- **Unbind** destroyed a 10 HP ward battery at R6T51 with no answer available (Counterbind/Phase Shift both unprepped or unfunded that round) — the same battery-fragility finding as m22.

### Anomalies / engine notes
1. **Reactions do not consume cast slots** — Mana Drain fired multiple times per round with zero effect on the `slots0/3` counter; only spell CASTS from the main stack economy consume slots. Worth confirming this is intended (it materially changes reaction-suite cost accounting from the doctrine's implicit assumption).
2. **Multiple simultaneous Wards are possible** — Fortify and Aegis Charm produced two separate ward objects (`wards[9,4]` etc.) rather than merging into one pool; Warding Tithe added a third independent ward. Ward-targeting effects (Unbind, Ward Collapse) appear to pick one at a time, which may matter for battery-management tactics not explored this game.
3. **Fatigue reshuffle damage reproduced exactly as m22 documented**: reshuffling an empty deck cost 2 direct HP (R8T73, deck hit 0 then reshuffled to 37, costing 2 HP at the worst possible moment — HP7 to HP1 in the same event as a 4-damage doom landing). This remains a high-impact, easy-to-overlook mechanic; a deck-count watch should probably be standard doctrine going forward, especially when HP is low.
4. **Grounding's "prevent 1" has no effect against prophecy-inscribing spells** — directly confirmed by testing (R1T5): the reaction fires (draws a card) but the prophecy's damage value is unaffected. This is consistent with, and now empirically verifies, the brief's "dooms pierce" premise at the reaction-interaction level, not just the arrival-damage level.
5. **Sealed Verdict's un-castability produces zero opportunity cost beyond 1 S/round** — because S components are abundant and it was always immediately detach-rescued, keeping it prepped across 3 rounds cost essentially nothing except prep-slot space that could have gone to a 7th denial tool. The real cost of running it is the prep slot, not the component fuel.
