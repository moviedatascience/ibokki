## Analysis

RESULT: greedy Evocation def. Divination (piloted), round 5, P0 -1 / P1 6 HP. Seed 6303. Game 3 of 3 in the NEW-DECK calibration series — first Div-side loss of the series; bot-level baseline for this edge is 100% Evo at ~5.6 rounds, so the round count landed right on the bot-level clock, but the margin (Evo finishing at 6/30 HP, one unanswered nuke from being reversed) shows this is a far closer race under the new decks than a bot-level "degenerate" edge implies.

DOOM-CLOCK LINE — SURVIVES INTACT: every prescribed element fired essentially as briefed. Omen cast on my very first turn of all 4 rounds it got to run (R1–R4), each time funded by exactly one MM-shaped attachment (one CMP-MM card, or one CMP-M + one CMP-VM/M pair) — the "easier now" prediction held completely; I never once failed to fund or cast Omen on schedule, and all 4 dooms resolved for 2 pierce each (8 total). Foretell landed every round (R1–R4, 4x, 8 direct dmg + hand-intel reveals that correctly flagged Evo's hand as thin/component-light in R1). Anticipate fired twice (R1 vs Spark, R3 vs Burning Hands) for 1 dmg + draw each — it never fired in R2 or R4 because I had no funded components on the turn a cast happened (VM-only or gambit-only hands), confirming the doctrine's "near-zero wasted M is the bar" is a real, hard-to-hit target even with the easier duals. Premonition on the 3-slot round (R3) dug first as prescribed and found a free Saboteur's Kit (GAM-019, played twice total across R3/R4 for 2 pierce each = 4 more). Foresight was underused until the last gasp at HP1 (R5) where its -1/spell reduction turned Hex Bolt's 3 into 2 — correct defensive read, just one HP short of surviving. The doom-clock chip (Omen+Foretell+2xSaboteur's Kit+Anticipate = 8+8+4+2 = 22 of P1's 24 damage taken) is what put Evo at 6 HP by R4 end — the plan objectively worked; it just didn't finish before Evo's own clock did.

EVO CLOCK SPEED vs OLD REFERENCES — MEASURABLY FASTER, ZERO BRICKS: this is the calibration's headline finding. Across all 5 rounds Evo funded and cast on literally every turn it had a cast slot available — no passed turns with an empty/dry hand, no wasted slots on unusable buffs, no missed casts. Compare to the m4 reference (old decks): there Evo passed R2-T5 with a dry hand (no V), burned its last R4 cast slot on Catalyst (a round-buff with zero remaining casts to use it — a real valuation blunder enabled by resource scarcity), and bricked BOTH of its R5 attack turns (hand 0), which was explicitly why that game's kill line closed out safely. None of that repeated here. Every single Evo turn produced a funded spell: Firebolt x4 (6 with Battle Trance R1, then 3/3/3), Spark x2 (2/2), Burning Hands x2 (1+1 burn each), and in R5 the L2 upgrades Inferno Lance (4) and Hex Bolt (3, reduced to 2) — six back-to-back damage turns with no gaps. Damage-to-me per round: R1 -8, R2 -3, R3 -7, R4 -7, R5 -6 (fatal). The one relatively quiet round (R2, -3) was a single Firebolt with no follow-up cast, not a brick — Evo simply didn't draw a second funded play, it wasn't punished for a bad decision. Net: the brief's prediction is confirmed — the new 8-VV-dual Evo build essentially eliminated the funding brick that padded the old piloted wins (m1-m4 went 4-0, m16-m18 went 2-1 partly on the back of exactly these bricks).

OMEN CADENCE: 4/4 rounds (R1–R4), always cast on my first turn of the round, always fully funded same-turn with either 1x CMP-MM or a 2-component M+M/VM pair. Zero missed rounds, zero funding failures — cleanly hit the "one MM card pays it" easier-cadence prediction from the brief.

ANOMALY — Counter-Plan vs "cannot be the target of Reactions" (Hex Bolt, EVO-012): this is the loss's proximate cause and looks like a genuine rules bug, not a misplay. At HP1 in R5, Foresight had reduced Hex Bolt's incoming damage from 3 to 2 (still lethal), so I fired Counter-Plan (DIV-024, "return target component attached to [the cast spell] to their hand; if the spell no longer meets its cost, cancel it") to strip Hex Bolt's single funding CMP-VV and cancel it outright — the single-card-funds-the-whole-cost pattern that the new duals made possible is exactly what makes this reaction so strong on paper. The engine allowed the react action (react-div-024) and resolved it without error, but the opponent's prep listing still showed the VV component attached afterward, and Hex Bolt fully resolved for its full (Foresight-reduced) 2 damage — killing me. Hex Bolt's own text says only "this spell cannot be the target of Reactions," and Counter-Plan's effect targets the *attached component*, not the spell itself, so by RAW text the interaction should have worked; as implemented it looks like the protection check is guarding the wrong object (or blocking the reaction's effect silently rather than blocking the reaction from being declared at all), producing a reaction that is legal to declare but mechanically inert against exactly the card type (a single-card-funded VV nuke) it exists to counter. Recommend: either make Hex Bolt-style "no Reactions" protection also make component-targeting reactions illegal to declare (so the player isn't baited into wasting their one out), or fix the resolution so the component-strip actually executes when the reaction is legal. Worth a `grep -rn "cannot be the target of Reactions"` / Counter-Plan implementation check — this is exactly the "SIMPLIFIED/proxy condition" bug pattern the doctrine flags.

SKILL VS RACE READ: the doom-clock playbook executed almost flawlessly — full Omen cadence, full Foretell cadence, correct Premonition-first sequencing, a defensively-alert Foresight cast at the last possible moment, and a theoretically-correct Counter-Plan read that should have won the game outright. The loss came from (a) Evo's new-deck funding reliability removing the brick-driven margin that carried the last two piloted wins, and (b) what appears to be an engine-level interaction bug that nullified the one defensive line that should have closed the gap. This reads as a genuine near-miss rather than a doctrine failure: Div pushed Evo to 6/30 HP (80% down) on a clock that matched the bot-level round count, and lost by exactly the margin of one broken Reaction.
