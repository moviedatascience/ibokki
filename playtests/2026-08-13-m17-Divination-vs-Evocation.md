## Analysis

RESULT: Divination (piloted) def. greedy Evocation, round 5, HP 14 vs -1 (15-point margin). Seed 5102. Post-fix re-validation of the m1-m4 doom-clock line against the tier-1/2-improved greedy instrument: the line still wins, and more comfortably than either m1-m4 reference game (which won at 2 HP and 6 HP margins).

PER-CARD CONTRIBUTION (opponent taken from 30 to below 0, 31+ dmg dealt):
- Dooms: Omen x2 (R1 T1, R4 T18) for 4; Saboteur's Kit x3 — one drawn in the R1 opening hand (played immediately, free), two more found by Premonition in R4 — for 6; Foreclosure (R5 T22, timed off a 7-HP read) for 4. Total scheduled/fired doom damage: 14.
- Foretell: cast successfully 4 of 5 attempts (R1, R2, R4 x2) for 8 dmg + hand intel each time; blocked twice this game by a legality bug (see ENGINE OBSERVATIONS) when the opponent's hand was empty, both times unblocked itself one turn later once they drew.
- Anticipate: fired 3 times (R1, R2, R4) for 3 dmg + 3 draws; missed its arm window in R2 when the opponent held first seat that round (matches the known geometry from the reference briefing).
- Premonition: cast twice (R3, R4). R3's draw was two plain M (no bonus find, no free gambit) — notably weaker than both m1-m4 reference draws. R4's draw was the big one: Saboteur's Kit + Sealed Vault, effectively re-loading the whole game (second free doom clock + discard-pile fuel refresh in one draw).
- Foresight: cast once (R3) for scry-3 + 1 dmg-reduction-this-round; the R4 casting attempt got fuel-committed (MM attached) but never got a free cast slot before the round ended, so that investment was recovered (detached) rather than wasted.
- Sealed Vault: played once (R4), refreshed the resource deck from 19 to 36 cards — big fuel-density save exactly when needed.

EVO PRESSURE ASSESSMENT vs the m1-m4 reference: mixed, net LOWER pressure than expected. On one hand the improved bot hit HARDER per-spell — a Battle Trance-buffed Firebolt did 6 damage in R2 (vs 3 in the unbuffed reference cases), suggesting the self-buff valuation fix is working as intended (no longer wasting Battle Trance on suboptimal targets). On the other hand, the bot fully bricked round 3 (zero casts across both its R3 turns, hand ballooning to 3 unused cards) — a harder, more complete brick than anything in the m1-m4 reference, which directly contradicts the pre-game expectation of a "less brick-prone" bot. Total damage taken by me across 5 rounds was only 16 (30→14), well under the ~23-24 the reference pilots absorbed. Net: this instrument's Evo was not meaningfully faster or more consistent than the one m1-m4 beat; if anything the sample looks softer, likely seed-dependent (V-component drought hit them hard in R2-R3) rather than a real instrument-strength signal.

VERDICT ON THE EDGE: the Div doom-clock racing line still comprehensively beats greedy Evo post-fix. This is game 2 of a 3-game series; on this seed the win was less contested than the 4-0 reference, not more. No evidence the tier-1/2 valve changes (slot-waste penalty, sweep-aware fuel pricing, detach-rescue) closed the gap from the Divination side in this game — if anything the opposing bricking got worse, not better. Recommend the 3rd game in the series to see if that holds.

PRICING NOTES:
- Confirmed via direct in-game testing (Design_Doc §Component System): a same-symbol double cost (Omen/Foreclosure's MM) can ONLY be paid by one same-symbol dual (CMP-MM) or two plain basics (M+M) — a plain M plus a cross-dual (SM/VM/VSM) does NOT validate, even though the cross-dual visibly carries an M symbol. This makes cross-duals functionally dead cards for topping off Divination's MM-cost spells; they're only useful funding single-symbol M costs (Anticipate/Foretell/Foresight/Premonition), where a lone cross-dual (with its other symbol simply wasted) funds fine. In a resource deck seeded with plenty of cross-duals, this is real friction against a mostly-pure-M Divination curve — it directly caused Omen to miss its R3 cast entirely this game (fuel arrived in the wrong shape at the wrong time, and by the time a second plain M showed up all 3 of the round's cast slots were already spent elsewhere).
- Clarified action economy (useful for future pilots, not previously documented this precisely): each turn allows exactly ONE spell cast (consuming one of the round's `slots` budget) AND separately up to one gambit/trainer play (free, doesn't touch the slots budget) — trying a second cast or a second gambit-play in the same turn is illegal even when both are fully funded/available. This cost real tempo this game: at R4 T16 I cast Premonition, found a second Saboteur's Kit + Sealed Vault, played the Kit, but the attempt to also play Sealed Vault and cast the now-funded Omen in the same turn both failed, and passing (with no stack item) ended the turn outright rather than returning priority — losing a full turn's worth of otherwise-ready value. Pilots should budget exactly 1 cast + 1 gambit-play per turn, not per available resource.

BOT BLINDSPOTS: greedy Evo's total R3 brick (0 casts, 2 turns, hand grew to 3 unused cards) is a strong data point that fuel-density valuation is still exploitable — the bot held cards without playing anything even fully passing on cheap V-cost Spark-tier spells it had previously shown willingness to cast partially-funded. Also again replaced its opener suite for bigger late spells (Spark→Inferno Lance, Firebolt→Hex Bolt) at R5 exactly as the m1-m4 reference did, but never got to cast either before dying — the swap was wasted tempo against a locked kill clock, consistent with the reference's "wasted final-slot investment" pattern.
