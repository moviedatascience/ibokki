## Analysis

HYPOTHESIS: Does kill-before-R10 still work now that Abj can convert its Reckoning bank into wards/heals/cancels midgame? VERDICT: Bank-starving remains highly effective at suppressing Reckoning itself (it stayed a near-non-factor throughout — 1 dmg, then 1 dmg again — thanks to active starving: holding casts against full walls, Dispelling Powder removing a ward for free, and consistently spending big spells only when face was clean). BUT the ledger family's *defensive* spenders (Restoring Rune healing 5, Second Wind, Warding Tithe rebuilding walls) combined with a DEEP cancel/counter suite (Sealed Verdict, Abjure the Wicked, Interrupt, Total Negation) repeatedly denied Evocation's alpha strikes outright, and a completely unanticipated OFFENSIVE tech — Overcharge (ABJ-036, sacrifice all your own Wards, deal 2 dmg per HP to opponent) — one-shot 14 damage into me from a mere 7 HP of banked ward, dropping me from 16 to 2 HP in a single spell. That swing, not Reckoning, decided the game: Evocation was racing a 15-HP opponent from 2 HP for three more rounds, survived two separate Reckoning casts (1 dmg each, confirming bank-starving worked on THAT axis), but died to a THIRD Reckoning tick when Final Riposte wasn't re-armed in time (attachments don't survive round boundaries, and the opponent's first action next round was an immediate Reckoning before I got a main-phase window to rearm defense).

RESULT: Loss, round 13, HP 0 (P0) vs 6 (P1). Very close race — opponent was at 4 HP two rounds earlier and I could not close it after the Overcharge swing put me in a permanent no-error state.

DECISIVE LINES:
- R10 T75: cast Sunburst (VVV, destroy-all-wards-into-face-damage) into an armed Abjure the Wicked — got cancelled AND took 4 damage from the cancel itself (2 per attached component). Lesson: multi-component big spells are riskier to cancel-punish decks than assumed; the SS-cost cancel suite doesn't just deny the spell, several variants (Abjure the Wicked) actively punish the caster.
- R10 T78: Overcharge (offensive ward-sacrifice, 2 dmg/HP to opponent) hit me for 14 off their own 7-HP ward stack. This is the mirror-image of my own Sunburst plan and I had zero prepared answer to it — Evocation's kit has no damage-prevention reaction at all, only post-hoc reflection (Final Riposte), which cannot stop lethal/near-lethal single hits, only punish non-lethal ones.
- R12 T97 and R13 T98: back-to-back Reckoning casts. First one (bank≈1-2, dealt only 1 dmg) got doubled back via Final Riposte for a 2-dmg counter-hit — validates that when the bank IS starved, Reckoning is genuinely toothless and reflectable. Second one landed clean because attachments reset at the round boundary and I had no main-phase window to rearm Final Riposte before their first action.

LEDGER-FAMILY TELEMETRY:
- Warding Tithe (ABJ-046): cast at least 5 times, converting 4 bank into a 4-HP ward each time — this IS a genuine bank sink, and combined with my starving discipline, kept the bank from ever ballooning; Reckoning maxed out at 1 damage per cast all game (would have been dramatically worse unchecked).
- Sealed Verdict (ABJ-047): cancelled one Fireball (spent 6 bank) — a real bank sink AND tempo swing.
- Restoring Rune (ABJ-048): observed being prepared/armed repeatedly but I did not see it resolve a big heal this game (healed 3 once, mid-game) — moderate impact.
- Reckoning (ABJ-032): cast 3 times total (R10, R12, R13), damage was 1/1/1 — the starving plan suppressed this wall to near-irrelevance the whole game, confirming the doctrine works even better than the old reference expected.
- Overcharge (ABJ-036) and Abjure the Wicked (ABJ-026): NOT part of the "ledger family" per the brief but are the actual game-deciding tech this game — both are offensive spends that convert Abjuration's defensive investment (wards, attached components) into direct damage against Evocation. These are the real blind spot, not the ledger spenders.

BLIND SPOTS / BUGS:
- No suspected rule bugs found; Interrupt (ABJ-013) stripping components and refunding a card draw, Overcharge's self-ward-sacrifice math, and Final Riposte's doubled-reflect all behaved exactly as printed.
- Design note (not a bug): Evocation currently has literally zero damage-prevention tools (no wards, no "prevent" reactions) — only after-the-fact reflection (Final Riposte) and cancel-avoidance (holding hand). Against an opponent with an offensive ward-sac payoff (Overcharge) this is a real structural gap: Abjuration can point its own defensive investment at Evocation's face for direct, unavoidable burst, while Evocation has no symmetrical tool and must win the whole game before that combo comes online (Overcharge is L3, available from ~R7-8 given typical leveling).
- Reshuffle/exhaustion penalty (2 dmg per reshuffle) hit both players roughly evenly (R9 mutual reshuffles); did not decide the game but added meaningful chip in a game already this close.
