## Analysis

RESULT: P0 (Divination) wins on HP at round 11, turn 85. Final HP: P0 28, P1 -3 (killed by a Foreclosure doom for 4 while at 1 HP). Fully manual play throughout — every decision hand-piloted, no autoplay used at all.

BANK TRAJECTORY: Abjuration's prevention bank stayed near-empty the entire game. Every ward P1 built (sizes 1-6 HP across ~12 wards total) was removed via Prophecy of Collapse (free destroy, recast every ~2 rounds) or Unbind (free destroy + draw, recast whenever funded) rather than chipped down with ordinary damage. The only bank credit I ever detected came indirectly: Stone Stance's "reduce damage by 2" absorbed a Saboteur's Kit doom for 2, and that 2 apparently fed the bank enough for one Warding Tithe to make a 2 HP ward later. Warding Tithe (max 4) and Restoring Rune (max 6, heal half) were both prepared by P1 but never mattered — bank never exceeded ~2-4 the whole game, so the "ledger family" the brief warned about was completely starved. P1 never cast Restoring Rune at all (nothing to spend).

SHATTER EVENTS: Never actually observed a genuine partial-absorb shatter (ward HP > incoming doom damage) because I pre-emptively popped wards with Unbind before the paired doom fired whenever I had the choice, converting "shatter for cheap bank credit" into "zero bank credit + doom hits flesh instead." The one clean case where a doom met a ward that could have shattered (Foreclosure vs. a Reflective Ward/4HP under Stone Stance) actually got reduced 4→2 by Stone Stance and then the ward had already been removed by my Unbind first, so it landed on flesh for 2. Net effect: I never let the opponent's bank collect anything from a doom-shatter — the "sequence Unbind-then-let-the-follow-up-doom-hit-flesh" line dominated over "let the doom shatter the ward."

HYPOTHESES:
1. BANK STARVING — strongly confirmed. Across 11 rounds and roughly a dozen ward-creation events, the bank was functionally dead; Warding Tithe/Restoring Rune were prepared but starved out.
2. SHATTER ECONOMICS (trade-up) — confirmed in principle (Prophecy of Collapse/Foreclosure destroy outright regardless of ward size, crediting 0), but in practice the even-better line was to pop the ward with Unbind first and let the smaller doom hit flesh directly — destroy > shatter > ordinary-damage-into-ward as a value ordering.
3. Scheduling Prophecy of Collapse/Foreclosure against their battery — confirmed as the core engine: recast every time it was off "cast this round" cooldown, destroyed a ward for free essentially every cycle (at least 6 free full-ward destructions across the game).
4. Tech answers: Stone Stance (spell-damage -2/round) worked as designed and reduced two of my doom hits, but only ever to a smaller positive number, never fully to 0 after round 4 (once the reduction was smaller than the doom). Total Negation (SSS reaction) successfully cancelled my Entropy doom when I baited it in at 3 HP — a good trade for me since it drained their strongest defensive reaction before the real kill sequence. Counterbind (SM, "cancel spell needing M component") was prepared but never actually got the chance to fire — I kept the M-heavy spells moving fast enough it seemingly never got fully funded/used. Sanctum/Aegis Eternal (L3, SSS) were drafted into P1's prep but never actually cast — too expensive/too late.
5. Doom clock vs. wall — the doom clock won comfortably, finishing at round 11 (target was ~R12), with me never dropping below 28 HP the entire game (P1's Abjuration kit generated essentially zero offense against me — their spells are almost entirely defensive/economy, so once bank-starved they had no clock of their own).

BOT BLIND SPOTS / RULE NOTES FOUND:
- Multi-symbol components (SM, VSM, SS) do NOT satisfy pure single-letter cost requirements (M, S) the way I expected from V/M working for M-cost — SM/VSM never enabled a cast attempt on M-cost Divination spells despite containing an M pip; only components whose pip-set is exactly the needed type (or a strict superset drawn from the SAME letter, e.g. MM covering M, or VM covering M) seemed to work. This needs engine verification — it's inconsistent with "extra pips just get wasted" and cost me several turns of fumbling.
- Attachment count per spell prep slot appears capped at 2 regardless of the spell's total cost — I could never fund the MMM-cost Entropy with three separate single-M cards; it only became castable once I had one card contributing 2 M-pips (an MM component) plus one single M, i.e. two attachments totalling 3 pips. This is a real constraint on level-3 spells when your hand is single-symbol-heavy; worth flagging to engine team as either working-as-intended (2-attachment cap) or a bug.
- One-spell-per-turn resets exactly at turn boundary, independent of the round's total-spell-slot budget (3 per round at this level) — confirmed working as documented in the brief, just noting it consumed real turns figuring out ("cast" flag column is per-round, not per-cast-ever).
- Deck exhaustion is real and symmetric: I hit it once (round 8, cost 2 HP via the reshuffle-and-take-damage rule) and P1 hit it too later (round 10, also took the exhaustion tax) — worth noting for anyone tracking how draw-heavy Divination lines interact with a long grind against a slow Abjuration deck.

Overall the leg strongly supports the pilot-gap doctrine: with careful sequencing (destroy > shatter, pop-before-the-paired-doom-lands, holding damage spells during Stone Stance windows, baiting Total Negation with a low-stakes doom before the real kill), Divination's unraveling suite completely dismantled the Abjuration ledger engine and never let it come online, in contrast to the 73% bot-level Abj win rate cited in the brief.
